# Delaware Entity Operating in Georgia vs. Forming in Georgia

> General information for founders, not legal or tax advice. Confirm specifics with a CPA or attorney before acting.

## Short answer
Where you form your company (Delaware) and where you do business (Georgia) are separate questions. If you have an office or employees in Georgia, your Delaware company must also register in Georgia as a "foreign" entity. That means you pay fees and keep a registered agent in **both** states. Delaware gives you **no Georgia tax savings**: Georgia taxes you based on your Georgia activity, wherever you were formed. Delaware is worth the extra cost for a C-corp that plans to raise venture capital. For a Georgia-based LLC that isn't raising VC money, a Georgia LLC is usually simpler and cheaper.

## By stage
- **Pre-revenue founders:**
  - **Choosing an entity:** if you plan to raise venture capital, a Delaware C-corp is the market default. If you're bootstrapped or not raising equity, a Georgia LLC means one state, one registered agent, a $100 formation fee and $50 a year ([§ 14-11-1101](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-1101/)). Forming in Delaware gives no Georgia tax savings.
  - **Delaware C-corp franchise tax:** before paying the March 1 bill, recalculate with the Assumed Par Value Capital method. A company with 10,000,000 authorized shares can see a bill of about $85,165 that recalculates to $400 ([DE tax calculator](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/)).
  - **GA registration:** a Delaware entity with a GA office or GA employee should register in Georgia as a foreign entity ($225). An unregistered LLC or corporation owes a $500 penalty after 30 days ([§ 14-11-711(c)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-711/); [§ 14-2-1502(b)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1502/)).
  - **LLC that may raise later:** converting to a C-corp earlier can matter for QSBS, which applies only to C-corp stock ([26 U.S.C. § 1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202)).
- **Funded teams hiring 2–10:**
  - A first GA hire triggers GA withholding registration with DOR ([GA DOR: Register a new business](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia)). Treat a GA employee as triggering foreign registration with the GA Secretary of State too.
  - Calendar the recurring deadlines: Delaware corporations March 1, Georgia annual registration January 1 – April 1, Delaware LLCs June 1, and registered agent renewals in both states.
  - Georgia taxes a corporation on its Georgia activity: 4.99% corporate income tax plus the net worth tax, up to $5,000 ([GA DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/business-taxes/corporate-income-and-net-worth-tax)).
  - An unregistered foreign entity can't maintain a lawsuit in a GA court until it registers ([§ 14-11-711](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-711/); [§ 14-2-1502](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1502/)).
  - For QSBS, the gross-asset limit is $75M for stock issued after July 4, 2025 ([§ 1202(d), (i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202)).

## What you need to know

**Formation state vs. doing business.** A foreign LLC or corporation "transacting business" in Georgia must get a certificate of authority from the GA Secretary of State ([O.C.G.A. § 14-11-702(a)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-702/) for LLCs; [§ 14-2-1501](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1501/) for corporations). The statute doesn't define "transacting business." It lists things that do **not** count on their own: keeping a bank account, holding owner meetings, selling through independent contractors, a single isolated transaction, interstate commerce, and owning a GA subsidiary ([§ 14-11-702(b)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-702/)). The statute says that list "is not exhaustive."
- **Practical rule:** a GA office or a GA employee is regular, local business activity and isn't on the safe list. Treat either one as triggering registration. This is our reading of the statute, not a line it draws itself. Your first GA hire also triggers GA withholding registration with DOR ([GA DOR: Register a new business](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia)).

**GA foreign registration.**
- Filing fee: **$225** for an application for a certificate of authority, for both corporations and LLCs ([§ 14-2-122](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-122/); [§ 14-11-1101](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-1101/)). Secondary sources report a small online service fee and a higher paper-filing fee on top of that ([LegalZoom](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-register-a-foreign-entity-in-georgia)).
- **Home-state certificate:**
  - **Corporations** must attach a certificate of existence from Delaware ([§ 14-2-1503(b)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1503/)).
  - A georgia.gov FAQ says foreign entities need an original Certificate of Existence/Good Standing "less than 90 days old" ([GNPEC FAQ](https://gnpec.georgia.gov/georgia-corporate-registration-faq)).
  - **For LLCs this is unconfirmed.** The § 14-11-702(a) application contents we reviewed don't list it, and secondary sources conflict ([Harbor Compliance](https://www.harborcompliance.com/foreign-georgia-llc) says it isn't required). Plan to get one anyway and check the form instructions.
- A Delaware short-form good standing certificate is reported at **$50** (secondary: [LegalZoom](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/delaware-certificate-of-good-standing)).

**Delaware ongoing costs.**
- **LLC annual tax:** **$400** under HB 400, applying from the **2026 tax year**. It's due **June 1**, so the first $400 payment is generally the one due June 1, 2027. The 2025-year payment was $300. There is no LLC annual report. The late penalty is $200 plus 1.5% interest per month ([DE alt-entity tax instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/alt-entitytaxinstructions/); [Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/delaware-businesses-face-increased-annual-taxes-and-filing-costs/)). *Note: older guides say $300, which is out of date.*
- **C-corp franchise tax + annual report:** due **March 1**. The annual report fee is $50 for non-exempt corporations. The late penalty is $200 plus 1.5% interest per month ([DE: Pay taxes](https://corp.delaware.gov/paytaxes/)). HB 400 did not change corporate franchise tax ([Delaware Inc.](https://www.delawareinc.com/blog/delaware-franchise-tax-update-hb-400/)). You pay the **lesser** of two methods ([DE tax calculator](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/)):
  - **Authorized Shares method.** This is the default on the bill.
    - $175 for up to 5,000 shares.
    - $250 for 5,001–10,000 shares.
    - +$85 per additional 10,000 shares.
    - Maximum $200,000.
  - **Assumed Par Value Capital method.**
    - Assumed par = total gross assets (from Form 1120 Schedule L) ÷ total issued shares.
    - Tax = $400 per $1M (or part of $1M) of assumed par value capital.
    - Minimum $400.
- **The scary bill.** Startups often authorize 10,000,000 shares. Under the Authorized Shares method, that's about **$85,165**. A company with $100K in gross assets and 8M issued shares at $0.00001 par can recalculate with the Assumed Par method: assumed par is $0.0125, capital is $125K, and the tax is **$400**. Don't pay the first number. Recalculate, or have your CPA do it, and file with your issued shares and gross assets ([DE tax calculator](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/)).
- **Delaware registered agent:** required. The agent needs a Delaware street address and must be available during business hours to accept legal papers. The state doesn't regulate agents ([DE: Registered agents](https://corp.delaware.gov/agents/)). Commercial agents charge an annual fee, typically around $50–$300. That range is our estimate, not from an official source.

**Georgia ongoing costs.**
- **Annual registration: $50.** It's due **January 1 – April 1** each year, starting the year after you form or register ([§ 14-2-1622](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1622/); [§ 14-11-1101](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-1101/)). A late fee of about $25 is reported (secondary — verify at https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-file-annual-registration).
- **GA registered agent and office:** required for any registered foreign LLC ([§ 14-11-703](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-703/)). Corporations have the same requirement under Title 14, ch. 2. That statute wasn't checked as of 2026-10-25.

**Taxes: Delaware doesn't help you in GA.**
- Georgia corporate income tax applies to corporations that "own property, do business in Georgia, or receive income from Georgia sources." The rate is **4.99%** of GA taxable net income ([GA DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/business-taxes/corporate-income-and-net-worth-tax)).
- **GA net worth tax** also applies to corporations doing business in GA. Net worth of $100K or less owes no tax but must still file. The maximum is $5,000 ([GA DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/business-taxes/corporate-income-and-net-worth-tax)).
- A Delaware LLC taxed as a partnership or sole proprietorship passes its income through. Owners pay GA income tax on their GA-source income. The GA DOR corporate income and net worth tax page doesn't address LLCs directly, so confirm the details with a CPA.
- Delaware doesn't tax income earned outside Delaware, but GA still does. Forming in Delaware adds costs and doesn't reduce Georgia tax.

**Penalties for not registering in GA.**
- **No lawsuits:** an unregistered foreign LLC or corporation **cannot maintain a lawsuit in a GA court until it registers.** Its contracts stay valid, and it can still defend itself if sued ([§ 14-11-711](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-711/); [§ 14-2-1502](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1502/)).
- **LLCs:** owe all the fees they would have paid, plus a **$500** penalty if still unregistered 30 days after they start doing business ([§ 14-11-711(c)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-711/)).
- **Corporations:** owe a **$500** civil penalty if not registered within 30 days ([§ 14-2-1502(b)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-1502/); [§ 14-2-122](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-2-122/)). The Secretary of State says statutory penalties cannot be waived ([GNPEC FAQ](https://gnpec.georgia.gov/georgia-corporate-registration-faq)).

**When Delaware is worth it, and when it isn't.**
- **Delaware C-corp:** the right default if you plan to raise venture capital. Investors, their lawyers, and standard financing documents expect it, and Delaware corporate law and courts are well established and predictable. *This is general market practice, not from a statute.*
- **Georgia LLC:** simpler if you're bootstrapped, a services or lifestyle business, or not raising equity. You get one state, one registered agent, one annual filing: a $100 formation fee ([§ 14-11-1101](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-1101/)) and $50 a year.

**LLC to C-corp conversion (if you'll raise later).**
- Delaware allows an LLC to convert directly into a Delaware corporation ([8 Del. C. § 265](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/index.html)).
- **QSBS (Qualified Small Business Stock):** under IRC § 1202, a federal tax break on gain from selling stock in a qualifying C-corp.
  - The tax break applies only to C-corp stock ([26 U.S.C. § 1202(c)–(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202)). LLC units don't qualify.
  - When you convert, the LLC's assets count at fair market value for the gross-asset test. The limit is $75M for stock issued after July 4, 2025 ([§ 1202(d), (i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202)).
  - Converting earlier can matter for QSBS.
- Converting a **Georgia** LLC into a Delaware corp is possible but needs an attorney. Converting later also costs legal and tax work, which is one reason VC-track founders often start as a Delaware C-corp.

## Steps
1. **Decide your entity.** If you're raising VC, form a Delaware C-corp. If not, form a GA LLC at [GA SOS online services](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-guide-online-services). You get formation documents back.
2. **If you formed in Delaware, order a Delaware good standing certificate** at [corp.delaware.gov](https://corp.delaware.gov/). You get a certificate. File in GA while it's under 90 days old.
3. **Hire a GA registered agent.** You get an agent name and GA street address for your application.
4. **File for GA foreign registration ($225)** through [GA SOS business services](https://sos.ga.gov/index.php/corporations), before you open a GA office or hire in GA, or at most within 30 days. You get a certificate of authority.
5. **Register with GA DOR** for withholding and income tax at [Register a new business](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia). You get a GA tax account. See also [GA withholding](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/withholding-tax-employers).
6. **Calendar recurring deadlines.**
   - Delaware C-corp: March 1.
   - Georgia: January 1 – April 1.
   - Delaware LLC: June 1.
   - Registered agent renewals in both states.
7. **C-corp: recalculate franchise tax** using the Assumed Par Value method before paying ([DE calculator](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/)).

## When to bring in a professional
- **Corporate attorney:**
  - Choosing Delaware vs. Georgia when you're about to raise money.
  - Converting an LLC to a C-corp, especially a GA LLC into a DE corp.
  - You already did GA business without registering, or you need to sue someone in GA.
- **CPA:**
  - A Delaware franchise tax bill over a few hundred dollars.
  - QSBS planning.
  - Multistate income tax.
  - LLC tax elections.
