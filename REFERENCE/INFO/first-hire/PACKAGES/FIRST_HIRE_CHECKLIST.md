# First-Hire Checklist for a Georgia Startup

> General information for founders, not legal or tax advice. Confirm specifics with a CPA or attorney before acting.

## Short answer
Set up your accounts before you make the offer. You need an EIN from the IRS, a Georgia withholding number from DOR (Georgia Tax Center), and a Georgia DOL unemployment insurance (UI) account. Get workers' comp lined up, and put up the required posters. The offer letter should be at-will and paired with an IP assignment agreement. On day 1 the hire completes the I-9, W-4, and G-4. You report them to the Georgia New Hire Reporting Program within 10 days. After that, payroll runs on a fixed schedule of tax deposits and quarterly filings. Most founders hand that to a payroll provider.

## By stage
- **Pre-revenue founders:**
  - Confirm the worker is an employee, not a contractor, before anything else (EMPLOYEE_VS_CONTRACTOR.md).
  - **Now:** set up the accounts before the offer: a free EIN from the IRS, a GA withholding number from DOR (usually within 15 minutes), and line up the GA DOL UI account, which is filed immediately after the first GA payroll ([IRS: Get an EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number); [GA DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia)). A Delaware or other out-of-state entity operating in Georgia generally has to register in GA as a foreign entity first (DELAWARE_ENTITY_IN_GEORGIA.md).
  - **Workers' comp counts founders:** corporate officers and LLC members are included in the 3-employee count, so two founder-officers plus a first hire can already be three ([SBWC Insurance FAQs](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs)).
  - **Can wait:** E-Verify is optional for a private GA employer with 10 or fewer employees, though you may need to file the exemption affidavit with your business license ([GA AG affidavit](https://law.georgia.gov/document/document/ag-699956-v1-privateemployerexemption10orfewer-after7113pdf/download)).
- **Funded teams hiring 2–10:**
  - Repeat the offer and day-1 phases for every hire: offer letter, PIIA, I-9, W-4, G-4, and a new-hire report within 10 days (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/dol224.pdf` p.24).
  - Above 10 full-time employees (35+ hours a week, counted as of January 1), E-Verify is required to get or renew a business license (O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6(b); `dol224.pdf` p.23). Some cities and counties require it at lower headcounts.
  - The board approves each equity grant (EQUITY_FOR_EARLY_HIRES.md).
  - Confirm in writing which deposits, quarterly filings, and new-hire reports your payroll provider files for you.
  - Remote hires in other states need an employment attorney, and remote teams should confirm with counsel how to deliver posters electronically (`remote-and-out-of-state-hires`).
  - Laws that start at 15, 20, and 50 employees are covered in HEADCOUNT_TRIGGERS_AND_COMPLIANCE_CALENDAR.md.

## What you need to know
- **Confirm the worker is an employee before anything else.** See EMPLOYEE_VS_CONTRACTOR.md.
- **The EIN is free and issued right away online.** The IRS says "You never have to pay a fee for an EIN." The online tool needs the responsible party's SSN or ITIN ([IRS: Get an EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number)).
- **GA DOR needs a NAICS code for every business registration.** Once you register, the account number arrives by email "within 15 minutes" ([GA DOR: Register a new business](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia)).
- **When you owe GA UI tax.** A private employer is liable once it either (a) employs at least one person in 20 different calendar weeks, or (b) pays $1,500 or more in gross payroll in any quarter. New employers pay 2.7% (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/dol224.pdf` p.49, p.72).
- **Workers' comp counts the founders.** Georgia requires coverage once you "regularly employ three or more persons." Regular part-timers count. For corporations and LLCs, "the officers or members are included in the employee count" ([SBWC Insurance FAQs](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs)). That means two founder-officers plus a first hire can already be three.
- **Georgia is at-will.** "An indefinite hiring may be terminated at will by either party" (O.C.G.A. § 34-7-1). Our only copy of that text is a secondary summary ([Justia, 2020 code](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2020/title-34/chapter-7/article-1/part-1/section-34-7-1)). Federal anti-discrimination law still applies.
- **E-Verify in Georgia applies above 10 employees.** Private employers with more than 10 employees must use E-Verify to get or renew a business license. Only employees working 35+ hours a week count, measured as of January 1 of the year the affidavit is filed (O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6(b)). Employers with 10 or fewer sign an exemption affidavit instead (`dol224.pdf` p.23; [GA AG exemption affidavit, O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6(d)](https://law.georgia.gov/document/document/ag-699956-v1-privateemployerexemption10orfewer-after7113pdf/download)). Some cities and counties require it at lower headcounts (`dol224.pdf` p.23).
- **Background checks from a screening company fall under the FCRA.** Before ordering a report you need a stand-alone written disclosure and the person's written permission. Before rejecting someone based on the report, you send them a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report and the FTC Summary of Rights. After the decision, you send an adverse action notice ([FTC: Using Consumer Reports](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-employers-need-know)).

## Steps

### Phase 1: Before you hire (2–4 weeks before the start date)
1. [ ] **Get the entity in good standing, and register in GA if it was formed elsewhere.** A Delaware (or other out-of-state) entity operating in Georgia generally has to register as a foreign entity. *Where:* [GA SOS Corporations Division](https://sos.ga.gov/index.php/corporations). The site blocks automated access, so verify it manually. *You get:* a GA control number. *Timing:* depends on SOS processing, so check the [fees/expedite page](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/filing-fees-and-expedited-processing-document-filings) manually. Details are in DELAWARE_ENTITY_IN_GEORGIA.md.
2. [ ] **Look up your NAICS code.** *Where:* [census.gov/naics](https://www.census.gov/naics/). The site blocks automated access, so verify it manually. *You get:* a 6-digit code that GA DOR requires ([DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia)). *Timing:* minutes.
3. [ ] **Get an EIN, if you don't already have one.** *Where:* [IRS online EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). It is free, so don't use sites that charge. *You get:* the EIN right away plus a confirmation letter to print. *Timing:* one session, which times out after 15 minutes of inactivity.
4. [ ] **Register for GA withholding.** *Where:* the [Georgia Tax Center](https://gtc.dor.ga.gov/_/), which you log into yourself ([who must register](https://dor.georgia.gov/how-do-i-register-withholding-payroll-tax-number)). *You get:* a GA withholding account number by email, usually within 15 minutes ([DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/register-new-business-georgia)). *Timing:* before the first payroll.
5. [ ] **Open a GA DOL unemployment insurance account.** *Where:* the "Online Employer Tax Registration" link on [GA DOL Online Services](https://dol.georgia.gov/online-services). *You get:* a UI tax account number right away (`dol224.pdf` p.56). After that, set up the [Employer Portal](https://eresponse.gdol.ga.gov/idp/sso/employer/login) for quarterly filing and rate notices. *Timing:* when you become liable for GA UI (1 employee in 20 weeks, or $1,500 in payroll in a quarter). GA DOL says to file the DOL-1A application "immediately following the payment of the first Georgia payroll" ([GA DOL UI FAQ](https://dol.georgia.gov/faqs-employers/employers-faqs-unemployment-insurance), checked 2026-10-25). Have your payroll provider ready to file it right after the first pay run.
6. [ ] **Get workers' comp coverage.** *Where:* a carrier licensed in Georgia, usually through an agent or your payroll provider. You can check a policy at [SBWC coverage verification](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/online-employers-workers-compensation-coverage-verification). *You get:* a policy and the WC posters. *Timing:* it's required at 3+ employees, founders included. Getting it before your first hire is cheap insurance against a workplace injury.
7. [ ] **Pick a payroll provider.** A good provider calculates withholding, makes deposits, files the quarterly reports, and handles new-hire reporting. Ask which of these it actually does. Some still need you to create the agency accounts in steps 3–5 yourself. *Timing:* a week or more before the first payday, to allow for bank verification.
8. [ ] **Put up the workplace posters.** *Where:* [GDOL Required Workplace Posters](https://dol.georgia.gov/laws-and-rules/gdol-required-workplace-posters). GA requires DOL-810 (UI), DOL-154 (vacation), and DOL-4107 (equal pay), plus the SBWC posters WC-P1 and WC-BOR. Federal posters (minimum wage, EEOC, OSHA, and others) are listed on the same page. For federal requirements, use the [USDOL poster advisor](https://www.dol.gov/general/topics/posters). Remote teams: confirm with counsel how to deliver posters electronically.

### Phase 2: The offer
9. [ ] **Offer letter.** It covers title, pay, exempt or non-exempt status, start date, and a contingency on I-9 eligibility and any background check. Include at-will language consistent with O.C.G.A. § 34-7-1, and leave out promises of a fixed term or "permanent" employment. Have an employment attorney review your template.
10. [ ] **PIIA / IP assignment.** Get it signed before or on day 1 so that code and inventions belong to the company. No government filing is involved. Use an attorney-drafted form.
11. [ ] **Equity grant, if any.** The board approves the grant, and some deadlines are time-sensitive. See EQUITY_FOR_EARLY_HIRES.md.
12. [ ] **Background check, if you run one.** Follow the FCRA steps: a stand-alone written disclosure and written permission before ordering the report, a pre-adverse action notice before rejecting someone based on it, and an adverse action notice after the decision ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-employers-need-know)). Run it only after a conditional offer. Do not ask for work-authorization documents before hire (`dol224.pdf` p.40).

### Phase 3: Day 1 (and the next 10 days)
13. [ ] **Form I-9.** The employee completes Section 1 no later than the first day of work, and not before accepting the offer. You complete Section 2 within 3 business days after the first day, by examining original documents. For a remote hire, E-Verify employers in good standing can use the DHS alternative procedure (a live video review), and others can use an authorized representative (see `remote-and-out-of-state-hires`) (`i-9instr.pdf` p.2; [USCIS I-9](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9)). Use the 01/20/25 edition. Keep it for 3 years after hire or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is later (`i-9instr.pdf` p.6). The I-9 is kept on file, not submitted to anyone.
14. [ ] **E-Verify.** It is optional for a private GA employer at 10 or fewer employees. You may need to file the exemption affidavit with your city or county business license ([GA AG affidavit](https://law.georgia.gov/document/document/ag-699956-v1-privateemployerexemption10orfewer-after7113pdf/download)). Above 10 full-time employees (35+ hours a week, counted as of January 1), enroll at [e-verify.gov](https://www.e-verify.gov/), which you log into yourself.
15. [ ] **Form W-4 (federal).** Use the [2026 revision](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-4). Keep it on file and give it to payroll.
16. [ ] **Form G-4 (Georgia).** Use Rev. 06/03/26 ([DOR G-4](https://dor.georgia.gov/g-4-employee-withholding); `TSD_Employees_Withholding_Allowance_Certificate_G-4.pdf`). Without a G-4, you withhold as if the employee is single with zero allowances.
17. [ ] **New-hire report.** Report within 10 days of the hire date to the [Georgia New Hire Reporting Program](https://ga-newhire.com) (online, file upload, or mail). Legal basis: O.C.G.A. § 19-11-9.2 and 42 U.S.C. 653A (`dol224.pdf` p.24). No employer is exempt. Many payroll providers file this for you, so confirm that yours does.

### Phase 4: First payroll and ongoing
18. [ ] **Deposits.** Federal: in your first calendar year you are a monthly schedule depositor for Form 941 taxes (`p15.pdf` p.33), paid through EFTPS. Georgia withholding is paid through GTC on the schedule DOR assigns you.
19. [ ] **Quarterly filings.** Federal Form 941 is due Apr 30, Jul 31, Oct 31, and Jan 31, rolling to the next business day if the date falls on a weekend. For 2026 Q3 and Q4, that's 11/02/2026 and 02/01/2027 (`p15.pdf` p.12). GA Form G-7 is also quarterly. The 2026 Q3 G-7 is due 11/02/2026 (`2026 Employers_Tax_Guide.pdf` p.6). The GA DOL-4N (UI) is due on the last day of the month after each quarter ends (`dol224.pdf` p.78).
20. [ ] **Annual filings.** Give employees their W-2s by Jan 31, or by Feb 1, 2027 for tax year 2026, because Jan 31, 2027 is a Sunday. File GA Form G-1003 with the W-2s (due 2/01/2027 for tax year 2026), plus federal Form 940 (FUTA). Rates, wage bases, and deposit details are in GA_EMPLOYER_TAXES.md.

## When to bring in a professional
- **Employment attorney:** offer letter and PIIA templates, non-compete or non-solicit terms, background-check policy, remote hires in other states, and any doubt about contractor vs. employee status.
- **CPA or payroll specialist:** owners on payroll (S-corp salary), multi-state withholding, or a missed deposit or late filing.
- **Corporate attorney:** equity grants, 409A valuations, and fixing foreign registration or good standing for a Delaware entity.
- **Insurance agent:** workers' comp class codes, and whether to cover officers or file a WC-10 waiver.
