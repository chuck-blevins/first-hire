# Payroll Providers vs. PEOs vs. DIY, and Benefits Basics for Small Georgia Employers

> General information for founders, not legal or tax advice. Confirm specifics with a CPA or attorney before acting.

## Short answer
Most Georgia startups with 1–10 employees use a payroll service, and some use a PEO. With a payroll service, you're still the employer, and you still owe any tax it fails to pay. A PEO "co-employs" your team and bundles payroll, workers' comp and group benefits. Only an IRS-certified PEO (a CPEO) takes on federal employment tax liability for the wages it pays. Under 50 full-time-equivalent employees, no federal or Georgia law requires you to offer health insurance or a retirement plan, and Georgia requires no paid vacation or sick leave. If you do offer benefits, low-admin options for a small team are a QSEHRA or ICHRA (an allowance toward individual health coverage), a SIMPLE IRA or safe harbor 401(k), and commuter benefits. Tax credits offset part of the cost.

## By stage
- **Pre-revenue founders:**
  - **What matters now:** if founders are on W-2 payroll, a low-cost payroll service is usually enough. Running payroll yourself is possible but risky.
  - **What can wait:** benefits. A founders-only SEP-IRA can be set up as late as your tax return due date ([IRS SEP](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-sponsor/simplified-employee-pension-plan-sep)). Owners of LLCs and S-corps have special health-benefit rules (`paying-founders`).
- **Funded teams hiring 2–10:**
  - **Health:** competing for hires makes benefits matter. Choose a group plan, a PEO plan, or a QSEHRA or ICHRA.
  - **Retirement:** add a SIMPLE IRA or a safe harbor 401(k) while the startup credits are available.
  - **Headcount:** at 20 employees, COBRA replaces Georgia continuation. At 50 FTEs, the ACA mandate starts and QSEHRA eligibility ends (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`).

## What you need to know

**1. The three ways to run payroll**

| | DIY | Payroll service | PEO (co-employment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who's the employer for tax purposes** | You | You. The service files under your EIN | The PEO, for wages it pays. Federal liability shifts only if it's a CPEO |
| **What it handles** | Nothing: you calculate, deposit, file | Calculations, deposits, 941/940, W-2s, GA G-7 and DOL-4N, and usually new-hire reports. Benefits are often add-ons | All of a payroll service's work, plus HR support, workers' comp, and access to the PEO's group health and 401(k) plans |
| **Typical pricing (secondary, rough)** | Your time, plus penalty risk | About $20–$200/month base plus $2–$15 per employee per month ([QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/payroll/cost-of-payroll/); [Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/payroll-services-cost)) | About $40–$160 per employee per month, or 2–12% of payroll, before benefit premiums ([ADP, a PEO seller](https://adp.com/resources/articles-and-insights/articles/p/peo-what-is-the-cost-of-a-peo.aspx)) |
| **Main risks** | Missed deposits, penalties, and personal liability for unpaid withheld taxes | You owe any tax the service fails to pay | Leaving a PEO means re-papering benefits and switching unemployment accounts (point 3). If a PEO isn't certified, you can still owe its unpaid taxes |

*Legal basis:* an employer who outsources payroll "will generally remain responsible for those duties, including liability for the taxes," and can be "personally liable for certain unpaid federal taxes" (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/p15.pdf` p.53; [IRS: Outsourcing payroll duties](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/outsourcing-payroll-duties)).

**2. Certified PEOs (CPEOs)**
- **Liability:** a CPEO is treated as the employer "only for wages and other compensation paid... by the CPEO" (`p15.pdf` p.4, 54). For those wages, "the CPEO is solely liable." For non-worksite employees, the customer can also be liable ([IRS: CPEO customers](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/cpeo-customers-what-you-need-to-know)). With a non-certified PEO, you generally keep the liability (`p15.pdf` p.53).
- **Checking status:** the [IRS public listings](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/cpeo-public-listings) are updated quarterly and include suspended and revoked CPEOs. If certification is revoked, customers "may also be liable." The contract must show the CPEO's exact name and EIN.
- **Switching:** when a CPEO contract starts or ends, the CPEO and the customer are treated as successor and predecessor employers, so Social Security and FUTA wage bases don't restart ([26 CFR 31.3511-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/31.3511-1)). With a non-certified PEO they may restart. *That's an inference we didn't verify.*

**3. Georgia unemployment insurance (UI) with a PEO**
- **Whose account:** a GA PEO is "recognized as the employer" of its leased workers. It reports and pays UI under **its own** GA DOL account (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/dol224.pdf` p.50; [O.C.G.A. § 34-8-32(b)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-8-32/), FindLaw 2024).
- **The exception:** if the PEO doesn't post the required bond, or doesn't meet the legal definition, "the client... is the liable party," and the PEO reports under the **client's** account number (`dol224.pdf` p.50; [Rule 300-2-7-.07](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-300-2-7-.07)).
- **Experience:** the PEO doesn't take over clients' UI experience, and its rate reflects only its own (Rule 300-2-7-.07).
- **Leaving:** you go back to filing DOL-4N under your own account. Register one first if you never had one (`ga-employer-taxes`). The rule doesn't say what rate you get. Expect the 2.70% new-employer rate, *an inference we didn't verify*. We also couldn't confirm whether the $9,500 wage base restarts. The live GA DOL pages we checked (UI FAQ, taxes, power of attorney) don't mention PEOs.

**4. Health coverage**
- **No federal mandate under 50:** the ACA employer mandate applies only at 50 or more full-time employees plus full-time equivalents (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`). Georgia has no state individual mandate and no state employer mandate *(secondary only: [Vitable](https://www.vitablehealth.com/home-care-compliance/georgia/health-benefits-aca))*.
- **Small-group plans and SHOP:** Georgia runs its own exchange, Georgia Access. Its SHOP serves employers with up to 50 employees. You enroll through a certified agent or an insurer, not HealthCare.gov ([Georgia Access](https://georgiaaccess.gov/learn-more/options-for-small-businesses)), and you can start any time of year ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/choose-and-enroll/shop-marketplace-overview/)).
- **Small Business Health Care Tax Credit:** worth up to 50% of premiums, for 2 consecutive years. To qualify you need:
  - fewer than 25 FTEs, not counting owners and their families;
  - 2026 average wages under $68,200 (2 × $34,100; [Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.09](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)), with the credit shrinking above $34,100 and above 10 FTEs;
  - to pay at least 50% of the employee-only premium;
  - to buy through SHOP ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/small-business-health-care-tax-credit-and-the-shop-marketplace); [Form 8941 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8941)).
- **QSEHRA:** an employer-funded allowance that reimburses individual premiums and medical costs tax-free.
  - **Eligibility:** you must have fewer than 50 full-time employees including FTEs, and offer **no** group health plan to anyone.
  - **2026 caps:** $6,450 self-only and $13,100 family, prorated for partial years.
  - **Terms:** the same terms for all eligible employees. You may exclude people with under 90 days of service, people under 25, and part-time or seasonal workers. Reimbursements are tax-free only if the employee has minimum essential coverage ([Pub 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b); Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.63).
  - **Notice:** written notice 90 days before each plan year. A QSEHRA reduces the employee's premium tax credit ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/)).
- **ICHRA ("CHOICE Arrangement"):** available to any size employer, with no dollar cap. Employees must have individual coverage or Medicare. You can't also offer a group plan to the same class of employees. Terms are the same within a class, except amounts can vary by age (up to 3:1) and family size. Employees can opt out yearly, and notice is due 90 days before the plan year ([29 CFR 2590.702-2(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/2590.702-2)).
  - **The new name:** CMS rebranded ICHRAs as "CHOICE Arrangements" in September 2026, a marketing change, not a legal one *(secondary: [Becker's](https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/choice/cms-rebrands-ichra/))*. Georgia launched **Georgia Access for Business** to offer them on Sept 15, 2026 ([OCI](https://oci.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-09-15/insurance-commissioner-john-f-king-launches-georgia-access-business)).
- **HSA with an HDHP (2026):** contributions up to $4,400 self-only or $8,750 family, plus $1,000 at 55+. The plan's deductible must be at least $1,700 / $3,400, and out-of-pocket costs can't exceed $8,500 / $17,000. Employer contributions must be comparable across employees, or a 35% excise tax applies. A general-purpose FSA makes an employee HSA-ineligible ([Pub 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969); Pub 15-B).
- **Health FSA (2026):** up to $3,400, with a $680 carryover if the plan allows it (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.15). A shareholder owning more than 2% of an S-corp can't participate as an employee (Pub 15-B; `paying-founders`).
- **Continuation coverage:** COBRA applies at 20+ employees. Below 20, Georgia state continuation gives the rest of the month plus 3 more months on insured plans (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`; `firing-and-offboarding-ga`).

**5. Retirement plans** (2026 limits: [IRS COLA table](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/cola-increases-for-dollar-limitations-on-benefits-and-contributions); [IR-2025-111](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500))

| | SIMPLE IRA | Safe harbor 401(k) | SEP-IRA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who can use it** | 100 or fewer employees; no other plan | Any size | Any size |
| **Employee deferral (2026)** | $17,000 ($18,100 in certain plans); catch-up at 50+ $4,000 ($3,850 in those plans); ages 60–63 $5,250 | $24,500; catch-up at 50+ $8,000; ages 60–63 $11,250 | None (employer only) |
| **Employer contribution** | Required: a match up to 3% **or** 2% of pay for everyone | Required: 3% of pay for everyone, **or** a match of 100% of the first 3% plus 50% of the next 2%. Vests immediately | Optional each year, the same % for everyone eligible, up to 25% of pay / $72,000 |
| **Admin** | Low. No Form 5500. Set up by Oct 1 (new employers have an exception) | Highest. Form 5500 every year. No ADP/ACP testing | Lowest. No 5500. Can be set up as late as the tax-return due date |
| **Fits** | 2–10 people on a budget | Teams that want higher limits or plan to grow | Founders only, or very few employees |

Sources: [IRS SIMPLE IRA](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-sponsor/simple-ira-plan); [IRS SEP](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-sponsor/simplified-employee-pension-plan-sep); [IRS 401(k) overview](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-sponsor/401k-plan-overview); [Operating a 401(k)](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/operating-a-401k-plan). The $72,000 figure is the 415(c) limit. Other 2026 figures: a $360,000 compensation cap, $800 in pay to qualify for a SEP, and a $160,000 highly-compensated threshold.

- **Startup credits (SECURE 2.0, Form 8881):** for employers with 100 or fewer employees earning $5,000+, at least one non-highly-compensated participant, and no plan in the prior 3 years ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-startup-costs-tax-credit)).
  - **Startup costs:** 3 years of credit. With 50 or fewer employees, it covers 100% of costs, capped at the greater of $500 or the lesser of $250 × eligible non-highly-compensated employees or $5,000. With 51–100 employees, it's 50%.
  - **Auto-enrollment:** $500 a year for 3 years.
  - **Employer contributions:** up to $1,000 per employee, at 100% in years 1–2, then 75%, 50% and 25%. It excludes employees paid over $100,000 (the 2023 figure; we didn't verify the indexed amount).
- **Required auto-enrollment:** 401(k)s set up after Dec 29, 2022 must auto-enroll employees at 3–10%, rising 1% a year to a cap of 10–15%. This applies to plan years after 2024. It doesn't apply while the business is under 3 years old, or until 1 year after the first tax year in which it "normally employed more than 10 employees." SIMPLE IRAs and SEPs aren't covered ([proposed regs](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-01-14/html/2025-00501.htm)). Final regulations cleared White House review on Oct 16, 2026 and are still unpublished *(secondary: [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2026/10/secure-2.0-automatic-enrollment-regs-clear-white-house-review))*. Until they apply, a "reasonable, good faith interpretation" complies.
- **Georgia state auto-IRA:** we found none enacted. The 2025 "Peach State Saves" bill (SB 226) has a status **we couldn't confirm** *(secondary: [ASPPA](https://www.asppa-net.org/news/2025/2/auto-ira-on-georgias-mind))*. Employees in other states may fall under those states' rules (`remote-and-out-of-state-hires`).

**6. Other benefits**
- **Life insurance:** group-term coverage is tax-free up to $50,000. The cost of coverage above that is wages for Social Security and Medicare ([Pub 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b)). Georgia insurers write group coverage for groups as small as 2 (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/guide_for_business_insurance.pdf` p.28, 2019 edition).
- **Disability insurance:** short-term and long-term disability coverage is optional (same guide pp.31–32). Georgia has no state disability insurance program. Only CA, HI, NJ, NY, RI and Puerto Rico have one. *We confirmed this from secondary sources only.*
- **Workers' comp:** required at 3+ employees, founders included (`ga-employer-taxes`). PEOs often supply the policy.
- **PTO:** Georgia doesn't require private employers to give paid vacation or sick leave. GA DOL calls vacation "a matter of agreement" (`dol224.pdf` p.17), and local governments can't mandate leave ([O.C.G.A. § 34-4-3.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-4-3-1/)). Kin care applies at 25+ employees (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`). Payout of unused PTO at exit depends on your policy (`firing-and-offboarding-ga`).
- **Commuter benefits (2026):** up to $340 a month for transit or vanpool, and $340 a month for parking, tax-free. The bicycle exclusion ended after 2025 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.16; Pub 15-B).

### Decision table: picking an approach by stage
| Situation | Payroll | Health | Retirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-revenue, founders only on payroll | Payroll service, or DIY if one founder and a CPA reviews it | None required. Founders buy individual coverage (`paying-founders`) | SEP-IRA, or nothing yet |
| First 1–3 hires, tight budget | Payroll service | QSEHRA or ICHRA with a fixed allowance | SIMPLE IRA, or wait |
| 3–10 hires, competing for talent | Payroll service plus a broker, **or** a PEO (check that it's a CPEO) | Small-group plan (SHOP if you want the credit), PEO plan, or ICHRA | Safe harbor 401(k) using the startup credits, or SIMPLE IRA |
| Remote employees in several states | PEO or multi-state payroll service (`remote-and-out-of-state-hires`) | ICHRA works across states | 401(k), and check those states' auto-IRA rules |
| Nearing 20 or 50 employees | Re-price: leaving a PEO often saves money at scale (secondary-source view, not verified) | COBRA at 20. ACA mandate at 50, and QSEHRA ends | 401(k), with a 5500 audit at about 100 participants (not verified here) |

## Steps
1. **Register the accounts, or confirm in writing who does:** EIN, EFTPS, GA DOR withholding in the Georgia Tax Center, and a GA DOL UI account (`first-hire-checklist`; `ga-employer-taxes`). With a PEO, GA UI runs on the PEO's account, so ask which state accounts you still need.
2. **Give the provider access, and keep your own.**
   - **IRS:** the provider files Form 8655 as your reporting agent. Activate your EFTPS Inquiry PIN so you can watch deposits, and keep your own address on file with the IRS (`p15.pdf` p.53–54).
   - **GA DOL:** file a GDOL Power of Attorney, or the Limited version for UI tax only ([GA DOL POA](https://dol.georgia.gov/power-attorney-information)).
   - **GA DOR:** *we didn't verify how GTC third-party access works.*
3. **For a PEO, check CPEO status** on the [IRS listing](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/cpeo-public-listings), and check the name and EIN in the contract.
4. **Choose health coverage** with a licensed or Georgia Access-certified agent. Send QSEHRA or ICHRA notices 90 days before the plan year.
5. **Choose a retirement plan.** A SIMPLE IRA must be set up by Oct 1. Claim the credits on Form 8881.
6. **Write a PTO policy:** accrual, carryover, and payout at exit.
7. **Each January, re-check** your headcount (20 and 50) and the double-check table in this package.

### Questions to ask a payroll or PEO vendor
- Are you an IRS-certified CPEO? What's your exact legal name and EIN? Do you file aggregate 941s under your EIN or ours?
- Which filings do you make for us (941, 940, W-2, G-7, G-1003, DOL-4N, new-hire reports), and which do we make ourselves?
- Whose GA DOL account will our wages be reported under, and have you posted the Georgia bond?
- If we leave mid-year, what happens to our wage bases, our GA UI account, our health plan, and our 401(k)? How much notice do you need, and are there exit fees?
- Do you guarantee in writing to pay penalties caused by your errors?
- Can we see EFTPS deposits and state filings ourselves?
- What is the full price per employee per month, including benefits admin, year-end forms, off-cycle runs, and workers' comp? How much can it go up at renewal?
- Do you support employees in other states, QSEHRA or ICHRA, and SIMPLE IRA or safe harbor 401(k) plans?
- Who handles COBRA or Georgia continuation notices, and who files the Form 5500?

## When to bring in a professional
- **CPA:**
  - Choosing between a PEO and a payroll service, or switching mid-year (wage base restarts, UI account changes).
  - Claiming the health or retirement tax credits.
  - Benefits for owners of LLCs and S-corps.
- **Benefits broker** (licensed in Georgia, or Georgia Access-certified):
  - Comparing a group plan, a PEO plan, and a QSEHRA or ICHRA.
  - Participation and contribution rules.
  - COBRA and Georgia continuation administration.
- **ERISA counsel or a third-party administrator (TPA):**
  - 401(k) plan design, auto-enrollment compliance, Form 5500, and correcting missed contributions.
- **Employment attorney:**
  - Reviewing a PEO contract (indemnities, exit terms, who is the employer).
  - Writing PTO policies.
  - Benefits for remote employees in other states.
