# Headcount Triggers and Annual Compliance Calendar (Georgia Startups)

> General information for founders, not legal or tax advice. Confirm specifics with a CPA or attorney before acting.

## Short answer
With your first W-2 employee, wage-and-hour law (FLSA), Form I-9, OSHA's general safety duties, Georgia unemployment insurance (UI), Georgia's lactation law, and jury and voting leave all apply. Workers' comp kicks in at 3 regular employees, and founders who are officers or LLC members count toward the 3. The big federal steps after that are **15** employees (Title VII, ADA, GINA), **20** (ADEA, COBRA), **50** (FMLA and the ACA employer mandate) and **100** (EEO-1, WARN). Each law counts heads differently, so check the counting rule, not just the number. In Georgia, E-Verify is required once you have **more than 10 full-time** employees. It's enforced through your business license. The deadlines come in clusters: the end of January (W-2s, 1099-NECs, Q4 payroll returns), March 1 (Delaware corporations), mid-March (partnerships and S-corps), April 1 (GA annual registration), and mid-April (C-corps and individuals), plus quarterly payroll returns at each month-end after a quarter closes.

## By stage
- **Pre-revenue founders (no employees yet):**
  - **What matters now:** entity filings.
    - GA annual registration by April 1.
    - Delaware tax: March 1 for corporations, June 1 for LLCs.
    - Your city or county business license.
    - Your income tax return.
    - GA net worth tax, if you're a corporation.
    - 1099-NECs by January 31 for any contractor you paid $2,000 or more in 2026.
  - **What can wait:** none of the headcount laws apply until you have an employee. The exception is founders: an officer or LLC member can count as an employee for workers' comp ([SBWC FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs)), and a corporate officer who performs services counts for GA UI (`dol4e.pdf` p.2). Set up payroll accounts before the first paycheck (see FIRST_HIRE_CHECKLIST.md).
- **Funded teams hiring 2–10:**
  - **Every quarter:** the payroll returns (941, G-7, DOL-4N) become routine.
  - **Workers' comp:** applies at 3 regular employees.
  - **10 or fewer employees all of last year:** OSHA's injury log isn't required.
  - **Plan ahead for 11 and 15:**
    - **11 full-time employees:** E-Verify becomes mandatory at your next license renewal.
    - **15 employees:** federal discrimination law, including ADA accommodation, applies.
  - **At 20 employees:** COBRA replaces Georgia's shorter state continuation coverage. This change happens fast for teams that hire quickly.
  - **Benefit plans:** if you start a 401(k), plan on a Form 5500 every July. Small insured health plans are usually exempt from the 5500.

## What you need to know

**1. How headcount is counted.** There is no single headcount. The main rules:
- **EEOC laws:** count anyone on payroll, including part-time, seasonal and temporary workers. Owners and contractors don't count. You need the threshold number "for every workday in at least 20 calendar weeks" in the current or preceding calendar year, and the weeks don't have to be consecutive. Related companies may be combined ([EEOC: counting](https://www.eeoc.gov/employers/how-do-you-count-number-employees-employer-has)).
- **COBRA:** counts part-timers as fractions and looks at "typical business days" in the prior year ([26 CFR 54.4980B-2, Q&A-5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/54.4980B-2)).
- **ACA:** averages full-time employees plus full-time equivalents (FTEs) over the prior year ([IRS ALE page](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/determining-if-an-employer-is-an-applicable-large-employer)).
- **WARN:** generally excludes part-timers ([20 CFR 639.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/639.3)).
- **GA E-Verify:** counts only employees working 35 or more hours a week, as of January 1 of the year you file the affidavit ([O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6(b)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-36-local-government/ga-code-sect-36-60-6/)). Local forms can count differently: DeKalb's affidavit counts any W-2 worker. Follow your licensing office's form (see `local-business-licenses-metro-atlanta`).
- **GA workers' comp:** counts regular part-timers *and* corporate officers or LLC members, even officers who waive coverage ([SBWC FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs)).

**4. Not headcount-based, but annual.**
- **Form 5500:** due for retirement plans like a 401(k) regardless of size. Welfare plans (health, dental and so on) with fewer than 100 participants are exempt if they're unfunded or fully insured ([29 CFR 2520.104-20](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/2520.104-20); [IRS 5500 Corner](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/form-5500-corner)).
- **GA net worth tax:** applies only to *corporations* (C or S) that do business in GA, own GA property, or are registered with SOS. You file it even if you owe nothing ([DOR NWT FAQ](https://dor.georgia.gov/net-worth-tax-corporations-faq)). LLCs taxed as partnerships don't file it.

## Steps
1. **Count your heads each January 1.** Count three ways: total employees, full-time employees at 35+ hours (for E-Verify), and full-time plus FTEs at 30+ hours (for the ACA). Also note your peak headcount last year (for OSHA). A payroll report gives you all of these.
2. **When you cross a line, act before the next hire:**
   - **3:** buy workers' comp.
   - **11 full-time:** enroll at [e-verify.gov](https://www.e-verify.gov/) before your license renewal.
   - **15:** adopt an anti-harassment policy and an ADA accommodation process. *(This is practice, not a statute.)*
   - **20:** set up COBRA administration with your carrier or PEO.
   - **50:** add FMLA, the ACA, and 1095-C reporting.
3. **Put every row of the calendar below into a shared calendar**, with reminders 2 weeks ahead. Ask your payroll provider, in writing, which rows it files for you.
4. **Re-check the posters** each January with the [USDOL Poster Advisor](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/posters.htm) and the [GDOL posters page](https://dol.georgia.gov/laws-and-rules/gdol-required-workplace-posters).

## When to bring in a professional
- **Employment attorney:**
  - You're about to cross 15, 20 or 50 employees, especially with remote staff in other states, whose laws add their own thresholds.
  - You're planning a layoff of 25 or more people, or anything close to WARN levels.
  - You get a charge of discrimination or an FMLA dispute.
- **Benefits broker or ERISA counsel:**
  - Your first health or 401(k) plan, COBRA or state continuation administration, the ACA ALE calculation, and Form 5500.
- **CPA:**
  - Entity tax returns, extensions, estimated tax, and GA net worth tax.
  - Related companies whose headcounts may be combined.
- **Insurance agent:**
  - Workers' comp renewal, officer waivers (WC-10), and premium audits.
