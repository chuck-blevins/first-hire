# Firing and Offboarding an Employee in Georgia

> General information for founders, not legal or tax advice. Confirm specifics with a CPA or attorney before acting.

## Short answer
Georgia is at-will. A Georgia employer can end an indefinite employment for any reason that isn't illegal (O.C.G.A. § 34-7-1). Illegal reasons include discrimination, retaliation, and breaking a contract. Before you act, document the reason and apply your rules consistently. Get legal review if the person is in a protected class or recently complained. Give the Georgia Separation Notice (DOL-800) on the last day. Pay final wages by the next regular payday. Pay out PTO only if your policy promises it. Severance is optional. If you offer it, get a written release, and follow the OWBPA rules for anyone 40 or older. Unvested equity is usually forfeited, and vested options usually must be exercised within about 90 days.

## By stage
- **Pre-revenue founders:** with even one employee, you owe the DOL-800, final pay on schedule, and I-9 retention. Anti-retaliation rules also apply. COBRA, WARN and the 25-person mass-separation notice can wait. Focus on documentation, a clean access cutoff, and getting company IP back.
- **Funded teams hiring 2–10:** expect a health plan, an option plan, and managers. Under 20 employees, Georgia state continuation stands in for COBRA. Use a separation agreement template reviewed by counsel. Answer every unemployment claim on time to protect your UI tax rate. Separating 25 or more people at one site triggers the DOL-402.

## What you need to know

**1. At-will and its exceptions.**
- **The rule:** "An indefinite hiring may be terminated at will by either party" (O.C.G.A. § 34-7-1, [FindLaw, current as of 2024-03-28](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-7-1/), secondary). GA DOL adds "as long as it is not for an illegal cause" (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/dol224.pdf` p.43).
- **Discrimination:** Title VII and the ADA apply at 15 employees, and the ADEA at 20. Georgia's age statute applies at any size (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`).
- **Retaliation:** protected activity includes filing or joining a complaint, opposing discrimination on a reasonable belief, and asking for an accommodation ([EEOC](https://www.eeoc.gov/retaliation)). A charge is due within 180 days, or 300 days where a state or local agency enforces a similar law ([EEOC](https://www.eeoc.gov/time-limits-filing-charge)).
- **Whistleblowing and other statutes:** federal whistleblower laws and USERRA ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/termination)); Georgia jury service (O.C.G.A. § 34-1-3).
- **Concerted activity:** the NLRA protects non-supervisors who act together about work conditions, union or not ([NLRB](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/the-law/employees/concerted-activity); [29 U.S.C. § 152(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/152)).
- **Contract:** a fixed term, or wages "payable at a stipulated period," can imply a hiring for that period (§ 34-7-1). That's why offer letters say "at-will" (`first-hire-checklist`).
- **Public policy:** secondary sources say Georgia courts have refused to create a general public-policy exception, citing *Borden v. Johnson*, 196 Ga. App. 288 (1990) ([Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/easy-guide-georgia), secondary). We didn't read the opinion.

**2. Before firing.**
- **The unemployment test:** for a discharge, a Georgia employer must prove "a specific work-connected act of misconduct" close in time to the firing, and that the employee knew it could cost them the job. "Inability by itself is not misconduct" (`dol224.pdf` pp.102, 112).
- **Mistakes GA DOL lists:** no final warning; ignoring your own discipline policy; firing for a pile of incidents instead of a final one; waiting too long after it; asking for a resignation, which counts as a discharge; and giving inconsistent reasons (`dol224.pdf` pp.43–44, 112, 124).
- **What to keep:** the policy, proof the employee knew about it, the warnings, and firsthand witness statements.
- *Practice:* get legal review before firing anyone in a protected class, on or back from leave, or who complained recently.

**3. Final pay.**
- **Timing:** federal law doesn't require immediate final pay ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/wages/lastpaycheck)). We found no Georgia final-paycheck statute. The next regular payday is common practice (secondary only; same finding as `exempt-vs-nonexempt-pay-rules`). The semimonthly pay rule still applies (O.C.G.A. § 34-7-2; `dol224.pdf` p.15).
- **Deductions:** none without written authorization (`dol224.pdf` p.43).
- **PTO:** no statute requires payout, so your policy controls. GA DOL calls vacation "a matter of agreement" (`dol224.pdf` p.17), and secondary sources agree. One secondary source says accrued PTO counts as wages under § 34-7-2. We couldn't confirm that.

**4. The Georgia Separation Notice (DOL-800).**
- **Who and when:** give one to every separated worker, quits included, on the last day of work. If the worker is unavailable, send it within 3 days by email or U.S. mail (O.C.G.A. § 34-8-190(c); [Rule 300-2-7-.06](https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/300-2-7)).
- **Delivery:** GDOL now encourages electronic delivery and still accepts signed paper ([GDOL, 2026-10-07](https://dol.georgia.gov/blog-post/2026-10-07/employers-updated-rules-simplify-doing-business-gdol)). Keep proof of electronic delivery for 4 years (Rule 300-2-7-.06).
- **Content:** state the reason "fully and clearly," for example which rule was broken and what warnings were given. List severance, but not vacation pay or earned wages (`dol224.pdf` pp.92–94; [form](https://dol.georgia.gov/document/separation-notices/separation-notice-individual-dol-800/download)).

**5. How the stated reason affects unemployment.**
- **Eligible:** lack of work, or a discharge that wasn't for misconduct (`dol224.pdf` p.102).
- **Disqualified:** misconduct, or a quit without good cause connected to the work. The claimant must then earn 10× their weekly benefit amount in new work to requalify. That rises to 12× for assault or theft of $100 or less, and 16× for larger theft, sabotage, or damage of $2,000 or more (O.C.G.A. § 34-8-194(2); `dol224.pdf` p.114).
- **Severance:** severance blocks benefits for the weeks it covers if it exceeds the weekly benefit. Vacation payouts don't (`dol224.pdf` pp.115–116).
- **Your role in the claim:**
  - Answer the DOL-1199FF within 10 calendar days, or by the date on the notice, through [SIDES](https://dol.georgia.gov/use-employer-separation-sides-e-response), mail, or fax.
  - Send documents before the fact-finding interview.
  - Appeal within 15 days (`dol224.pdf` pp.106, 118, 126; [GDOL FAQ](https://dol.georgia.gov/faqs-employers/employers-faqs-unemployment-insurance)).
  - A late answer can leave your account charged even if you win later (p.118), which raises your rate (`ga-employer-taxes`).

**6. Mass separations and WARN.**
- **Georgia:** 25 or more employees at one establishment, separated the same day for the same reason, for 7 days or more. File the DOL-402 and DOL-402A within 48 hours, instead of individual DOL-800s (`dol224.pdf` p.96; Rule 300-2-7-.06).
- **Federal WARN:** 100 or more employees, 60 days' notice of a plant closing or mass layoff ([20 CFR 639.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/639.3)).

**7. Benefits on the way out.**
- **COBRA (20+ employees):**
  - The employer notifies the plan within 30 days.
  - The plan sends the election notice within 14 days, or 44 days if the employer is the administrator ([29 CFR 2590.606-4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/2590.606-4)).
  - The employee has 60 days to elect and 45 days to pay.
  - Coverage lasts 18 months, at no more than 102% of cost ([EBSA](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/an-employers-guide-to-group-health-continuation-coverage-under-cobra)).
- **Georgia continuation (insured plans, mostly under 20 employees):** the rest of the month plus 3 months. It doesn't apply after a termination "for cause" ([O.C.G.A. § 33-24-21.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-33-insurance/ga-code-sect-33-24-21-1/), secondary). Ask your carrier who sends the notice.
- **401(k):** leaving the job allows a distribution.
  - Balances over $5,000 need consent. A plan may raise that limit to $7,000.
  - Unclaimed forced cash-outs over $1,000 must be rolled into an IRA.
  - Direct cash payments have 20% withheld ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/401k-resource-guide-plan-participants-general-distribution-rules); [Notice 2024-2](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-24-02.pdf)).
  - Follow your plan document.

**8. Severance agreements.**
- **Optional:** severance is a matter of agreement ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/wages/severancepay)). Trade it for a release of claims, and pay something the employee isn't already owed.
- **OWBPA (40+, at ADEA employers):** the release must be plain-language and name the ADEA. It must advise seeing a lawyer, allow 21 days to consider (45 in a group program, with written disclosure of the decisional unit and ages) and 7 days to revoke, and not waive future claims. No agreement can bar an EEOC charge ([EEOC](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/qa-understanding-waivers-discrimination-claims-employee-severance-agreements)).
- **NLRB *McLaren Macomb* (2023):** broad confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses offered to non-supervisors violate the NLRA.
  - It's still Board law as of 2026-10-25. The Board applied it in *Prime Communications*, 374 NLRB No. 88 (2026-04-07), and two members said they're open to revisiting it ([NLRB](https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-april-6-10-2026)).
  - The General Counsel has asked the Board to overrule it ([Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/nlrb-general-counsel-issues-memorandum-asking-board-to-overturn-biden-era-precedent/), secondary).
  - Use narrow clauses for now.
- **Speak Out Act:** a non-disclosure or non-disparagement clause signed *before* a sexual harassment or assault dispute can't be enforced for that dispute. Trade secrets are unaffected ([Pub. L. 117-224](https://www.congress.gov/117/plaws/publ224/PLAW-117publ224.htm)). The Act mostly reaches offer letters and PIIAs, not settlements made after a dispute (secondary reading).
- **Georgia restrictive covenants (O.C.G.A. § 13-8-50 et seq.):**
  - Covenants are enforceable if written and reasonable in time, area, and scope.
  - Non-competes bind only salespeople, people who solicit customers, managers, and key employees or professionals.
  - Customer non-solicits can bind anyone, but only for customers they had material contact with.
  - A restriction of 2 years or less is presumed reasonable, and courts can narrow an overbroad one ([§ 13-8-53](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-13-contracts/ga-code-sect-13-8-53/); [§ 13-8-57](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-13-contracts/ga-code-sect-13-8-57/), secondary).
  - The FTC's non-compete rule is not in effect ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule)).

**9. Equity on departure** (details in `equity-for-early-hires`).
- **Unvested:** usually forfeited under the plan.
- **Vested options:** usually a 90-day exercise window ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/leaving-company/post-termination-exercise-period/), secondary). An ISO exercised more than 3 months after employment ends (1 year if the holder is disabled) is taxed as an NSO ([26 U.S.C. § 422(a)(2), (c)(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/422)).
- **Restricted or early-exercised stock:** the company usually may repurchase unvested shares at the lower of cost or FMV, often within about 90 days (secondary templates only). Calendar it.
- **LLC profits interests:** follow the operating agreement.

**10. IP and confidentiality.** PIIA duties survive the end of employment (`employee-vs-contractor`). Remind the employee in writing, and get a certificate that company data was returned or deleted. A confidentiality agreement signed or updated after May 2016 must include the DTSA immunity notice. Without it, you lose exemplary damages and fees against that employee ([18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833)).

**11. References.** When a prospective employer or the job seeker asks, a Georgia employer sharing facts about job performance, ability, or criminal-type acts is presumed to act in good faith. Proof of bad faith by a preponderance rebuts the presumption. It doesn't cover breaches of an NDA or of confidentiality law (O.C.G.A. § 34-1-4, [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-1-4/), secondary). *Practice:* one person handles references and confirms dates and title.

**12. Records after termination.**
- **I-9:** the later of 3 years after hire or 1 year after termination (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/i-9instr.pdf` p.6; [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/form-i-9-resources/handbook-for-employers-m-274/100-retaining-form-i-9)).
- **Payroll and tax:** FLSA payroll 3 years and time cards 2 years (`exempt-vs-nonexempt-pay-rules`). Employment tax records 4 years (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/p15.pdf` p.9). Georgia withholding records 4 years (`REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/2026 Employers_Tax_Guide.pdf` p.17).
- **Personnel files:** 1 year after termination, longer if a charge is filed ([29 CFR 1602.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1602.14); applies to Title VII and ADA employers).

**13. Contractors.** Ending a contractor relationship follows the contract's notice, final-invoice, and deliverable terms. The DOL-800, final-wage rules, and COBRA don't apply to a true contractor. Confirm the IP assignment covers everything delivered, revoke access, and file a 1099-NEC ($2,000 threshold for 2026; `headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`). Georgia's UI test presumes anyone you pay is an employee, so a contractor's unemployment claim can lead to reclassification (`employee-vs-contractor`).

## Steps
Offboarding checklist with timing:
1. **Weeks before:** gather the policy, the warnings, and the final incident. Check for protected class, leave, or recent complaints, and call counsel if any apply. Check headcount: 20 for COBRA and ADEA/OWBPA, 25 at one site for the DOL-402.
2. **1–3 days before:** prepare the final check, the DOL-800, the separation agreement, a benefits summary, and an equity statement (vested amount, deadlines).
3. **Meeting day:** two company people attend. Give a brief reason that matches the DOL-800, and deliver the DOL-800.
4. **Same hour, revoke access** (practice): SSO or IdP, email and forwarding, Slack, GitHub org membership plus deploy keys and tokens, cloud IAM keys, the password manager, CI/CD secrets, the DNS registrar, banking and payment tools, SaaS admin roles, and MDM lock or wipe. Rotate shared secrets and transfer document ownership.
5. **Days 1–3:** collect devices. Mail or email the DOL-800 if you couldn't hand it over. Send the PIIA reminder and get the data-return certificate.
6. **Within 48 hours (mass separation):** file the DOL-402 and DOL-402A.
7. **Next regular payday:** final wages, plus PTO if your policy promises it.
8. **Within 30 days:** notify the COBRA administrator or the carrier, and the 401(k) provider.
9. **By the date on the notice (10 days):** answer the DOL-1199FF. Appeal within 15 days.
10. **After revocation (day 8 after signing, for someone 40+):** pay severance.
11. **About day 90:** the exercise or repurchase window closes. Update the cap table.
12. **Afterward:** set retention purge dates. Issue the W-2 by Feb 1, 2027 for 2026 wages (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`).

## When to bring in a professional
- **Employment attorney:** before firing anyone who is in a protected class, on or back from leave, or who recently complained or asked for an accommodation. Also for any severance agreement (especially for someone 40+ or a group program), layoffs of 25 or more, non-compete enforcement, demand letters or EEOC charges, and employees in other states.
- **Corporate attorney:** share repurchases, extending exercise windows, acceleration, and LLC profits-interest departures.
- **CPA or payroll provider:** severance withholding, NSO exercises by departing employees, and final W-2s.
- **Benefits broker or ERISA counsel:** COBRA or Georgia continuation notices, and 401(k) distributions.
