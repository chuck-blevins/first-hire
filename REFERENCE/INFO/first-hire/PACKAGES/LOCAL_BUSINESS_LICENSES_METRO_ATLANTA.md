# Local Business Licenses and Occupation Tax in Metro Atlanta

> General information for founders, not legal or tax advice. Confirm specifics with a CPA or attorney before acting.

## Short answer
In Georgia, a "business license" is usually an **occupation tax certificate (OTC)**: a yearly local tax plus a registration. You file where your office or location is. Inside a city's limits, that's the city. In an unincorporated area, it's the county ([O.C.G.A. § 48-13-6](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-48-revenue-and-taxation/ga-code-sect-48-13-6/)).
- **Pre-revenue and home-based businesses** generally still register, and many pay a minimum tax (in DeKalb, a $75 fee plus a $50 minimum).
- **Home businesses** also need zoning approval.
- **E-Verify affidavit:** every application includes one. You either give your E-Verify number (more than 10 full-time employees on Jan 1) or sign the exemption.
- **Renewals** come due between Dec 31 and Apr 30, depending on where you are.

## By stage
- **Pre-revenue founders:**
  - **What matters now:**
    - Find out whether your city or your county covers your address.
    - Register before you start operating. Sandy Springs, for example, charges a penalty if you apply more than 30 days after starting (secondary).
    - Working from home? File the home-occupation form too.
    - Sign the "10 or fewer" E-Verify exemption affidavit.
    - Expect to pay a minimum tax plus an administrative fee.
    - A Delaware entity has to register in GA first (`delaware-entity-in-georgia`).
  - **What can wait:** E-Verify enrollment, and renewal until your first January.
- **Funded teams hiring 2–10:**
  - **The tax grows with you:** it usually scales with gross receipts or headcount, and you report both at renewal. Renewal falls in the same Jan–Apr window as W-2s and 941s (`headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`).
  - **Count employees every January 1.** At 11 full-time employees, enroll in E-Verify before you renew.
  - **When you move:** moving out of your home, or into a different city, usually means closing the old certificate and applying in the new jurisdiction. Home-occupation rules often bar staff on site.

## What you need to know

**1. What the occupation tax is.** Georgia defines it as "a tax levied on persons, partnerships, corporations, or other entities for engaging in an occupation," set by local ordinance to raise revenue ([§ 48-13-5](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-48-revenue-and-taxation/ga-code-sect-48-13-5/)). It's a tax on the business, not a deduction from wages (see `ga-employer-taxes`). Cities and counties are "authorized but not required" to levy it ([§ 48-13-6](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-48-revenue-and-taxation/ga-code-sect-48-13-6/)), and every jurisdiction in this package does. FindLaw's copies of these statutes are "current as of March 28, 2024."

**2. City or county.** A county taxes businesses "with one or more locations or offices in the unincorporated part of the county." A city taxes businesses with locations "within the corporate limits" (§ 48-13-6(a)–(b)).
- **Check the address, not the mailing city.** A "Decatur" or "Marietta" mailing address can be unincorporated.
- **Fulton County** licenses only the unincorporated Fulton Industrial District ([Fulton](https://fultoncountyga.gov/Inside-Fulton-County/Fulton-County-Departments/Finance/Business-Licenses)).
- **More than one location:** each location generally needs its own certificate ([Roswell](https://www.roswellgov.com/business/business-registration/)).
- **No GA location:** a business with no location in Georgia pays only the local government where it does "the largest dollar volume of business" ([§ 48-13-7](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-48-revenue-and-taxation/ga-code-sect-48-13-7/)).

**3. How the tax is calculated.**
- **What the statute allows:** employee count (full-time equivalents, with part-time hours ÷ 40), gross receipts combined with nationwide profitability ratios, or a flat fee, applied consistently to every business. An administrative fee may be added ([§ 48-13-10](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-48-revenue-and-taxation/ga-code-sect-48-13-10/)).
- **Tax classes:** in practice your NAICS code sets your tax class, and each class has a gross-receipts rate and a per-employee rate. Software and professional services usually land in the top class (see the [Brookhaven rate table](https://www.brookhavenga.gov/DocumentCenter/View/799/Occupational-Tax-Rate-Table-PDF), a 2014 file).
- **DeKalb example:** a $75 administrative fee, a $50 minimum tax, tax on receipts over $20,000, and a fee per worker, owner included ([DeKalb 2026 notice](https://dekalbcountyga.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/2026%20RENEWAL%20APPLICATION.pdf)).
- **Professionals:** lawyers, CPAs, engineers and similar professionals can often elect a flat per-practitioner fee instead (DeKalb and Marietta list $400).

**4. Pre-revenue still registers.** Cobb requires a certificate from "any individual, partnership, corporation/LLC, or entity engaged in business" ([Cobb](https://www.cobbcounty.gov/community-development/business-license/obtain-business-license)). Roswell makes even tax-exempt nonprofits register ([Roswell](https://www.roswellgov.com/business/business-registration/)). Minimum taxes and administrative fees, like DeKalb's, apply at $0 in receipts. Brookhaven cites businesses that operate without a current certificate, and Atlanta reportedly does too (secondary).

**5. Home-based businesses.** A home office still needs the certificate, and it adds a zoning step. Typical limits: no non-resident employees on site, few or no client visits, no signage, and a cap on floor area.

**6. E-Verify affidavit.** Private employers with **more than 10 full-time employees** (35+ hours a week, counted as of **January 1** of the filing year) must give their E-Verify user ID with every license application or renewal. At 10 or fewer, they sign the exemption affidavit ([§ 36-60-6](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-36-local-government/ga-code-sect-36-60-6/); `REFERENCE/INFO/first-hire/dol224.pdf` p.23). These figures match `first-hire-checklist` and `headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`.
- **Notary:** the affidavit is usually notarized ([DeKalb notice](https://dekalbcountyga.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/2026%20RENEWAL%20APPLICATION.pdf) p.5).
- **Local counting rules differ.** DeKalb's form counts any W-2 worker, with no hours test. Roswell counts "W-2 employees who work 35 hours or more per week" ([Roswell](https://www.roswellgov.com/business/business-registration/)). Near 10 employees, ask your office how it counts.
- **Lower local thresholds:** some jurisdictions require E-Verify below 11 employees (`dol224.pdf` p.23).
- **SAVE affidavit:** many offices also require one (O.C.G.A. § 50-36-1), with a copy of your ID.

**7. State professional licenses.** A local certificate doesn't replace a state license in a regulated field (for example nursing, architecture or cosmetology). Most of those are issued by the GA Secretary of State's Professional Licensing Boards. Accountancy and dentistry are among the exceptions ([SOS licensing boards](https://sos.ga.gov/page/georgia-licensing-boards), verify manually; [license lookup](https://verify.sos.ga.gov/verification/)). Local offices often ask for a copy of your state license with the application.

## Steps
1. **Confirm your jurisdiction.** Run your physical address (your home, if you work there) through the county address lookup, for example [Fulton](https://fultoncountyga.gov/Inside-Fulton-County/Fulton-County-Departments/Finance/Business-Licenses). *You get:* the one office from the jurisdiction table you file with.
2. **Clear zoning.** For a home business, use the home-occupation form. For an office, confirm the use is allowed and whether it needs a Certificate of Occupancy, and do this before you sign a lease. *You get:* zoning approval or an acknowledgement.
3. **Gather documents:**
   - your GA SOS registration
   - EIN
   - NAICS code
   - a government ID
   - any state professional license
   - an estimate of gross receipts and employee count
4. **Sign the affidavits:** E-Verify (exemption or your E-Verify ID) and SAVE. Many offices require a notary.
5. **Apply in the office's portal and pay.** *You get:* an occupation tax certificate.
6. **Calendar the renewal** from the jurisdiction table, alongside `headcount-triggers-and-compliance-calendar`, with a reminder 2 weeks ahead.
7. **Report changes:** a move, a new owner, a new line of business, or a closure. DeKalb requires this within 60 days. Certificates can't be transferred to a new owner.

### What to look up for any other GA city or county
1. Is the address inside city limits or unincorporated? Check the county GIS or address lookup.
2. What is the office called, and what are its URL and phone number?
3. What's the tax basis (receipts, employees, or flat)? Find the rate schedule or calculator URL, the administrative fee, the minimum tax, and whether there's a professional flat-fee option.
4. Is there a home-occupation form or permit? What does it limit?
5. Which affidavits are required (E-Verify, SAVE), and do they need a notary? Is there a local E-Verify threshold below 11?
6. When does the certificate expire? When are filing and payment due? What are the penalties and interest, and do weekend deadlines roll?
7. Is there a deadline for new businesses, or prorating for a mid-year start?
8. Is there an online portal, and does it need a PIN from a mailed notice?
9. Do you also need a state license or regulatory permit?

## When to bring in a professional
- **CPA:**
  - You have offices in more than one city, or large out-of-state receipts.
  - You need to apportion gross receipts. Georgia's Court of Appeals divided GA receipts across all offices nationwide in *City of Atlanta v. Block, Inc.* (2025) ([EY summary](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1519), secondary).
  - You're deciding on the professional flat-fee election, or facing a city audit.
- **Zoning attorney:** you need a variance or special use permit (such as Marietta's Type B), you'll have clients or staff at your home, or you've received a citation.
- **Employment attorney:** you're near 10 full-time employees and local and state counting rules differ, or you're unsure whether contractors count (`employee-vs-contractor`).
- **Corporate attorney:** your entity isn't in good standing with GA SOS, or ownership is changing.
