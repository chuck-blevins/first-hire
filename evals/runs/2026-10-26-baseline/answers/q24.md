General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Close the handbook for this question.** The GA DOL Employer Handbook (DOL-224, R-11-24) still prints pre-2019 federal figures: **$455/week** and a **$100,000** highly-compensated threshold. Those are out of date, so don't use them. Georgia has no overtime law of its own. The federal FLSA rules control.

**No salary, by itself, gets you out of overtime.** To be exempt, your developer has to pass **both** a pay test and a duties test. The job title doesn't decide it.

**Pay test (current federal figures, re-checked live today on [DOL's salary-levels page](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime/salary-levels)):**
- **Salaried:** at least **$684/week ($35,568/year)**. That works out to $1,482 semimonthly or $1,368 biweekly.
- **Hourly computer employee:** at least **$27.63/hour** ([WHD Fact Sheet #17E](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/17e-overtime-computer), re-checked today).
- The higher 2024 figures ($844 and $1,128) were struck down in court. DOL restored $684 in May 2026.

**Duties test (computer employee exemption, [29 CFR 541.400](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-541.400)):** their *primary duty* must be real systems analysis or software work, such as designing, building, testing, or modifying programs to specifications. These roles usually don't qualify:
- help-desk or IT setup
- QA that runs someone else's test scripts
- a junior or trainee developer who isn't yet doing that work

**Is there a level where they're "automatically" exempt?** Not quite. The closest is the **highly compensated employee** test. It requires **$107,432 or more** in total annual compensation, including at least $684/week paid as salary. The employee must also *regularly perform at least one* exempt duty ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime/salary-levels), re-checked today). For a real developer, that's an easy bar to meet, but it still isn't purely about pay.

**Stage-specific points:**
- **Being pre-revenue doesn't exempt you.** A developer who emails, calls, or works with people and systems in other states is very likely covered by the FLSA individually, even if your company has under $500K in sales ([WHD Fact Sheet #14](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/14-flsa-coverage)).
- **The $684 must be paid in cash, every pay period.** Equity doesn't count toward it, and neither does salary you "accrue" until you raise.
- **If you can't pay $684/week**, the role is non-exempt. Track hours, pay at least $7.25/hour, and pay 1.5× for hours over 40 in a week.
- **Pay at least semimonthly.** That's Georgia's rule.

**Next step:** write a short job description listing the primary duties, and state "exempt" or "non-exempt" in the offer letter. If the role mixes development with support or ops, or the developer is junior, ask an employment attorney before classifying them as exempt. Misclassification means back overtime for 2–3 years plus an equal amount in damages.
