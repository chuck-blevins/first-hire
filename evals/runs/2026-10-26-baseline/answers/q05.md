*General information, not legal or tax advice.*

**Budget about $9,480 a year (roughly 7.9%) on top of a $120K Georgia salary in 2027**, plus a workers' comp premium. All of these are paid by the employer and not withheld from the employee.

| Tax (employer share) | 2027 basis | Cost on $120K |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security | 6.2% on wages up to **$192,300** (2027 base) | $7,440 |
| Medicare | 1.45%, no cap | $1,740 |
| FUTA (federal unemployment) | 6.0% on first $7,000, less the 5.4% credit for paying GA UI on time = 0.6% net | $42 |
| GA unemployment (SUTA) | ~2.70% on first $9,500 (new-employer rate; see the 2027 caveat below) | ~$256.50 |
| **Total required** | | **≈ $9,478.50** |
| Workers' comp | Required at 3+ employees, founder-officers count | Get a carrier quote (usually modest for software roles) |

**Notes on these figures**
- **Social Security wage base.** SSA announced the 2027 base of **$192,300** on Oct 14, 2026, up from $184,500 in 2026 ([APA](https://www.payroll.org/news-resources/news/news-detail/2026/10/14/social-security-wage-base-increases-to-$192-300-for-2027); [Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/oct/social-security-wage-base-and-cola-announced-for-2027/)). It doesn't change this hire's cost because $120K is under either cap. I couldn't load ssa.gov directly, so confirm against the SSA fact sheet. Rates are in [IRS Topic 751](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751).
- **Additional Medicare tax.** The extra 0.9% on wages over $200K is employee-only. It doesn't apply here.
- **GA SUTA rate for 2027.**
  - New employers are at 2.70% on a $9,500 wage base ([GA DOL FAQ](https://dol.georgia.gov/faqs-employers/employers-faqs-unemployment-insurance)).
  - Through 2026, that rate is a 2.64% contribution plus a 0.06% assessment that expires Dec 31, 2026 under [SB 160](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2023-signed-legislation/sb-160/download). I couldn't confirm the exact 2027 total.
  - If you've been a GA employer long enough to get an experience-based rate, yours could differ. Use the rate on your DOL-626 notice in the [Employer Portal](https://www.dol.state.ga.us/public/uitax/emplportal/login). Either way, the swing is only tens of dollars per employee.
- **FUTA credit reduction.** The $42 assumes Georgia isn't a FUTA credit-reduction state. It hasn't been, but DOL posts the final list each November ([DOL](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/futa_credit.asp)). I couldn't read the list spreadsheets today.
- **Local payroll taxes.** No metro Atlanta city or county payroll tax was found. Occupation taxes are a business license fee, not a per-employee tax.

**What this doesn't include**
- Benefits: health, 401(k), PTO.
- Payroll provider fees.
- The equity grant.
- Your share of a 401(k) match, if any.

A common planning rule is to budget 1.25–1.4× salary for the fully loaded cost. That's an industry rule of thumb, not a legal figure.

**When to bring in a CPA:** if this hire is in a high-cost state, works remotely outside Georgia, or gets a large bonus that pushes them near the Social Security cap. Each of those changes the math.
