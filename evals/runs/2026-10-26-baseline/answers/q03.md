**No, you can't skip it.** The threshold is right, but founders *do* count. Once you hire the engineer, you'll almost certainly need workers' comp.

**The rule.** Georgia requires coverage if you "regularly employ three or more persons," part-time or full-time. For corporations and LLCs, "the officers or members are included in the employee count" ([SBWC Insurance FAQs](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs)). You and your cofounder are both officers drawing salaries, so the engineer makes **three**. Coverage needs to be in place by that start date.

**About the officer waiver.** Up to five officers can opt *themselves* out of coverage by signing Form WC-10 and filing it with the insurance carrier. The waiver doesn't help you here, though: SBWC says "officer exemptions do not reduce the number of employees" ([same FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs)). You'd still need a policy covering the engineer, so the waiver only lowers your premium.

**What happens if you skip it.**
- The State Board can impose civil penalties of **$500 to $5,000 per occurrence**.
- It can also add attorney's fees and a 10% increase in benefits to an injured worker.
- Willfully going without coverage is a misdemeanor ([SBWC Employer Information](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/employer-information)). That page is marked Rev. 7/15, so confirm current amounts with SBWC.
- And if the engineer gets hurt with no policy in place, the company pays out of pocket.

**What to do before the engineer starts.**
1. **Get a quote from a GA-licensed carrier.** Go through an insurance agent or your payroll provider; many bundle pay-as-you-go workers' comp. For a small software team the policy is usually inexpensive, but your quote depends on class codes and payroll.
2. **Decide whether you two want WC-10 waivers.** Talk it through with the agent.
3. **Post the required notices.** These are the Bill of Rights (WC-BOR), a Panel of Physicians (WC-P1 or WC-P3), and your insurer's name ([SBWC](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/employer-information)).
4. **Confirm the policy appears** in the [SBWC coverage verification lookup](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/online-employers-workers-compensation-coverage-verification).

**The same "founders count" logic shows up elsewhere.** As salaried corporate officers, you're already W-2 employees for payroll tax and Georgia unemployment insurance purposes. Make sure your payroll is set up that way, with a GA DOL UI account, if it isn't already.

**Ask a professional:** an insurance agent for class codes and the waiver decision, and a CPA if you're unsure your founder salaries are being run through payroll correctly.
