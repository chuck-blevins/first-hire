General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Short answer:** No 1099. Assuming she's a Polish resident and not a US citizen or green-card holder, and she does all the work in Poland, collect a **Form W-8BEN** from her instead of a W-9. A non-US person paid for work done entirely outside the US generally gets no 1099-NEC.

**Why no 1099**
- **Where the work happens decides the source.** The IRS says "the place where the services are performed determines the source of the income" ([IRS Pub 515](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515)). Work done in Poland is foreign-source income, so you generally don't withhold US tax on it.
- **The W-8BEN replaces the 1099.** A valid Form W-8 lets you treat her as a foreign payee who is exempt from Form 1099 reporting and backup withholding (Pub 515).
- **Caveat:** the IRS doesn't say "no 1099-NEC and no 1042-S" in a single sentence. That conclusion is our reading of Pub 515, and I couldn't re-read that specific section live today. A CPA can confirm it for your situation.

**What to collect from her**
1. **Form W-8BEN.** The current revision is October 2021 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-8-ben), checked today). She fills it out and gives it to you, and you keep it on file. You don't send it to the IRS. If she invoices through a company rather than as an individual, collect a W-8BEN-E instead.
2. **A written contract with an IP assignment**, signed before she writes code. A contractor's code isn't automatically yours, and US copyright transfers must be in writing ([Copyright Office Circular 30](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf)). Polish law may limit how rights transfer or keep certain author's rights with her, so have the assignment clause reviewed by someone who knows Polish law. *(I haven't verified Polish law.)*
3. **An OFAC screen.** US companies must comply with sanctions. Run her name through [OFAC Sanctions List Search](https://sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov/) and keep the result.

**Things to watch**
- **Is she really a contractor?** Polish law decides whether she counts as an employee there. About 20 hours a week, with her own schedule and tools and other clients, looks like a contractor. Working full-time only for you, on your hours, looks like an employee. If it becomes full-time and long-term, compare an Employer of Record (EOR), such as Deel or Remote (examples, not endorsements), with keeping her as a contractor.
- **US visits:** work she does while physically in the US is US-source income, and different rules apply.
- **If she's a US citizen or green-card holder living in Poland,** the rules flip. Get a W-9 from her and send a 1099-NEC if you pay her $2,000 or more in 2026, due January 31 ([1099-NEC instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec), checked today).
- **Export controls:** sharing source code with a foreign person can be a controlled "deemed export" if your product involves encryption or is defense-adjacent ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov/learn-support/deemed-exports/what-deemed-export)). Most SaaS products are fine, but check if that applies to you.

**At your stage:** a contract with an IP assignment, a W-8BEN, and an OFAC screen are the minimum. An EOR can wait.
