**Probably not, if the product is pure cloud access, but confirm this with a sales-tax CPA.** My first-hire materials don't cover sales tax yet, so the information below comes from a fresh search and isn't from a vetted package.

**What I found:**
- **Georgia's sales tax mainly applies to tangible goods and a list of specific services.** The Georgia Department of Revenue has issued letter rulings finding that **cloud subscriptions, where customers access the vendor's software over the internet and never receive a copy, are not taxable**. Examples are [LR SUT 2014-01](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/sut-lr/lr-sut-2014-01/download) and [LR SUT 2014-05](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/sut-lr/lr-sut-2014-05/download).
- **Caveats:**
  - I found these rulings through search and couldn't read their full text today, so the summary above is from secondary sources.
  - Letter rulings bind only the taxpayer who asked for them, on those facts.
  - They're from 2014. I didn't find a current DOR statement that confirms or replaces them.
- **What can change the answer:**
  - **Downloadable or installed software:** if customers download software, or you ship anything on a physical medium, Georgia can treat that as a sale of taxable software.
  - **Bundled hardware or tangible items:** these can be taxable too.
  - **Invoices:** secondary sources say DOR presumes tangible delivery if your invoices don't show electronic delivery. Make sure your invoices and contracts say "accessed remotely / delivered electronically."

**Registration threshold (if anything you sell is taxable):** secondary sources report that a remote seller has to register in Georgia once it has more than **$100,000** in Georgia sales, or **200 or more** separate sales, in the current or prior calendar year. I couldn't confirm this on dor.georgia.gov. If you have an office or employees in Georgia, you already have a physical presence there, so the threshold doesn't matter.

**Other states:** whether SaaS is taxable varies a lot from state to state. If you have customers elsewhere, a nexus review of every state you sell into is worth doing now, before the exposure grows.

**Next steps:**
1. Bring a short product description to a **state and local tax (SALT) CPA**: what customers get, how it's delivered, and any add-ons such as implementation or training.
2. If the answer is still unclear, request your own letter ruling from the Georgia DOR.
3. If you do need to register, file through the [Georgia Tax Center](https://gtc.dor.ga.gov/) yourself.
