**Short answer:** For you, no. For your 10% cofounder, yes, probably. Federal wage law exempts an owner who holds at least 20% and actively manages the business, so you can go unpaid. At 10%, your cofounder doesn't get that exemption. If they work full time, they're likely an employee owed at least minimum wage in cash, and equity doesn't count toward it.

**Why the 20% line matters**
- **The exemption:** an employee who "owns at least a bona fide 20-percent equity interest" and is "actively engaged in its management" is an exempt executive, with no salary requirement ([29 CFR 541.101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/541.101), checked today). You fit that at 60%. Your cofounder doesn't at 10%.
- **Your cofounder is an employee.** In a C-corp, an officer who works in the business is an employee, and employees "may not volunteer services to for-profit private sector employers" ([DOL](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/docs/volunteers.asp)).
- **What they're owed:**
  - **Minimum wage:** the federal $7.25/hour. Georgia's $5.15 doesn't apply to employers covered by federal wage law ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage/state), checked today).
  - **Overtime** over 40 hours a week, unless they also qualify as a salaried exempt employee (which needs a salary of at least $684/week).
  - **Paid in cash.** Stock and options don't count toward either; wages must be paid "in cash or negotiable instrument" ([29 CFR 531.27](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.27)).
- **"We're pre-revenue" doesn't remove coverage.** A company with under $500,000 in sales isn't automatically covered, but any employee whose work regularly involves interstate commerce is ([DOL Fact Sheet 14](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/14-flsa-coverage)). Out-of-state email, cloud tools, and customers usually count. Treat your cofounder as covered.
- **The risk:** back wages, often doubled as liquidated damages, can surface later in a cofounder dispute or a fundraising due-diligence review.

**Options to discuss with an attorney**
1. **Pay your cofounder at least minimum wage through payroll**, or a modest salary, with equity on top. This is the simplest fix. Putting them on payroll means:
   - Register with the IRS (EFTPS), Georgia DOR (withholding), and Georgia DOL (UI).
   - Pay FICA and Georgia UI. Corporate officers who perform services are covered for UI.
   - A payroll provider can run this cheaply.
2. **Restructure ownership or roles.** Raising the cofounder to 20%+ with real management duties is an equity decision with tax and cap-table consequences. Don't make it just to avoid payroll; get counsel.
3. **Don't "accrue" salary as a workaround.** Accrued pay is still owed. It's taxed as wages when paid, can raise 409A deferred-compensation issues, and investors often ask founders to waive it at a priced round.

**While you're at it**
- **Founder stock:** make sure both of you have signed stock purchase agreements with vesting. If the stock is subject to vesting, file an 83(b) election within 30 days of the grant.
- **Your own pay:** whenever you do start paying yourself, as a C-corp founder that's W-2 payroll too.

**Bring in an employment attorney now** about the cofounder's unpaid status, and a CPA for setting up payroll.
