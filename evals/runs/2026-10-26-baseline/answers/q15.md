General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Short answer:** Her payroll taxes are North Carolina's, not Georgia's. Your company still has to register in Georgia, though, because you two run it from Atlanta.

**Her payroll: North Carolina**
- **Income tax withholding:** North Carolina requires withholding from residents' wages wherever the work is done. Register for an NC withholding number through NCDOR's online business registration (or Form NC-BR), then follow Form NC-30 ([NCDOR PD-10-1 and withholding guidance](https://www.ncdor.gov/documents/pd-10-1-withholding-nonresident-contractors-and-itin-holders)).
- **Georgia withholding doesn't apply to her.** Georgia withholds from nonresidents only for services performed in Georgia. That changes if more than 5% of her wages, or more than $5,000, comes from work in Georgia (for example, long visits to Atlanta) (GA DOR 2026 Employer's Tax Guide pp.9–10, 15, [download](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2026-employers-tax-guide-updated-june-2026/download); last verified 2026-10-25, not re-fetched today).
- **Unemployment insurance goes to NC.** Unemployment insurance (UI) follows where the work is "localized," and all of her work is in NC. You become liable to NC's Division of Employment Security after $1,500 in wages in a quarter, or one worker in 20 different weeks of a year. Register through NCSUITS or Form NCUI-604 ([NC DES](https://des.nc.gov/employers/am-i-required-pay-taxes)).
- **Federal is the same everywhere:** an EIN, W-4, I-9, FICA, and FUTA ([IRS Topic 751](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751): 6.2% Social Security on wages up to $184,500 for 2026, plus 1.45% Medicare, from each side; checked today).
- **NC employment law applies to her:** minimum wage (also $7.25), pay rules, posters, new-hire reporting to NC, and workers' comp rules. Check NC's workers' comp rules with an insurance agent. I haven't verified NC's threshold.

**Georgia: yes, the company still registers**
- **Secretary of State.** Two partners managing the LLC from Atlanta is regular, local business activity, so your Delaware LLC should register in Georgia as a foreign LLC. The statutory fee is $225. Plan on $50 a year after that and a Georgia registered agent. An LLC that stays unregistered more than 30 days owes a $500 penalty plus back fees, and it can't sue in a Georgia court ([O.C.G.A. § 14-11-711](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-14-corporations-partnerships-and-associations/ga-code-sect-14-11-711/)). sos.ga.gov blocks automated checks, so **confirm current fees yourself** at [GA SOS filing fees](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/filing-fees-and-expedited-processing-document-filings).
- **Georgia income tax:** you two report your Georgia-source share of profit, and the LLC files a GA partnership return.
- **No Georgia payroll accounts yet.** You don't need a GA withholding or GA DOL UI account until someone actually works in Georgia. That's because you two aren't payroll employees: in a multi-member LLC, partners take draws or guaranteed payments, not W-2 wages ([IRS: Partnerships](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/partnerships)).

**Probably also needed (ask a CPA):** registering the LLC in North Carolina as a foreign entity, and NC income tax nexus. An employee working there regularly can create both. I haven't verified NC's test.

**Next step:** a payroll provider that handles multi-state payroll will register you with NCDOR and NC DES and run her payroll. Have a CPA confirm the NC entity question before her first paycheck.
