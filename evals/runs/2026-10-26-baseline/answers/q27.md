General information, not legal or tax advice.

**"We take on the liability" is only partly true. It depends on whether they're IRS-certified, and on which liability they mean.**

**Federal payroll taxes.** Only a **Certified PEO (CPEO)** takes this on. For wages it pays to your worksite employees, "the CPEO is solely liable" for the employment taxes, deposits, and returns ([IRS: CPEO customers](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/cpeo-customers-what-you-need-to-know), re-checked today). Two exceptions:
- You can still share liability for anyone who isn't a "worksite employee."
- You can also share it if the PEO's certification is revoked.

With a **non-certified PEO**, or a regular payroll service, you generally stay responsible for any tax it fails to pay. You can even be personally liable for unpaid withheld taxes ([IRS: Outsourcing payroll duties](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/outsourcing-payroll-duties)).
- **Check status:** look the PEO up on the [IRS CPEO public listing](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/cpeo-public-listings). It's updated quarterly and lists suspended and revoked CPEOs separately.
- **Match the contract:** the legal name and EIN in your contract should match the listing.

**Employment-law liability.** This covers wage-and-hour issues, worker classification, discrimination, and how you fire people. Our sources don't show a PEO taking this off your hands, and as co-employer you still direct the work. Read the indemnity and exit clauses closely, and have an employment attorney review the contract before you sign. Benefits and workers' comp coverage do move to the PEO's plans.

**Georgia unemployment insurance, if you leave in 2 years:**
- **While you're with them:** a bonded Georgia PEO reports UI under **its own** GA DOL account. It does not take over your experience history. If the PEO doesn't keep its required bond, the reporting falls back to **your** account and name ([Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 300-2-7-.07](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-300-2-7-.07), re-checked today). Ask them in writing whether they've posted the Georgia bond.
- **When you leave:** you go back to filing DOL-4N under your own GA DOL account. Keep it open or know how to reactivate it, and set up a GDOL Power of Attorney for your next payroll provider.
- **Unverified:** what rate you get back. Expect something like the **2.70% new-employer rate**, rather than any good experience you'd built. GA DOL confirms 2.70% is the new-employer rate and $9,500 is the wage base ([GA DOL UI FAQ](https://dol.georgia.gov/faqs-employers/employers-faqs-unemployment-insurance), re-checked today). Neither GA DOL nor the rule says how a returning client is rated.
- **Unverified:** whether the $9,500 wage base restarts if you leave mid-year. Federal wage bases (Social Security, FUTA) carry over between a CPEO and its client. With a non-certified PEO, they may restart. **Leave at year-end** to avoid paying them twice.

**Questions to ask the PEO:**
- Are you a CPEO? What are your exact name and EIN?
- Whose GA DOL account will report us, and is your Georgia bond posted?
- What are the exit fees and the notice period?
- What happens to our health plan, 401(k), and workers' comp when we leave?
- Will you guarantee in writing to cover penalties caused by your errors?

Have a CPA model the switch, and an employment attorney review the contract.
