General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Your cofounder's reason is wrong, but the conclusion may be right.** Delaware **won't save you Georgia taxes**. Georgia taxes a corporation based on its Georgia activity, wherever it was formed. An Atlanta-based company pays Georgia's **4.99%** corporate income tax on Georgia taxable income, plus the Georgia net worth tax (none at $100K or less, though you still file; up to $5,000) ([GA DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/business-taxes/corporate-income-and-net-worth-tax), checked live 2026-10-26). Delaware doesn't tax income you earn outside Delaware, but Georgia still does.

**Why a Delaware C-corp still makes sense for you:** you plan to raise a seed round. A Delaware C-corp is the market default for venture-backed startups. Investors, their lawyers, and standard financing documents expect it, and Delaware corporate law is well established and predictable. That's general market practice, not a legal requirement. A C-corp also lets you grant ISOs, NSOs, and restricted stock, and only C-corp stock can qualify for QSBS ([26 U.S.C. §1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202)).

**What it costs you to be in both states:**
- **Georgia foreign registration (certificate of authority):** **$225** by statute. File before you hire or open an office in Georgia, or at most within 30 days. Unregistered corporations face a **$500** penalty and can't sue in Georgia courts until they register (O.C.G.A. §14-2-1502).
- **Georgia annual registration:** **$50**, due Jan 1 – Apr 1 each year.
- **Delaware franchise tax and annual report:** due **March 1**; the report fee is **$50** ([DE: Pay taxes](https://corp.delaware.gov/paytaxes/), checked live).
  - Delaware's default bill uses the Authorized Shares method. With the common 10M authorized shares, that can show around $85K.
  - Recalculate with the **Assumed Par Value Capital** method (minimum **$400**). Most early startups end up near the minimum ([DE calculator](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/)). Never pay the scary first number without recalculating.
- **Registered agents:** you need one in each state. Commercial agents charge roughly $50–$300/yr each (estimate, not official).
- **Delaware good standing certificate** to file in Georgia: about $50 (secondary).

I couldn't verify the Georgia Secretary of State fees ($225 and $50) live today, because sos.ga.gov blocks automated access. They come from the statute and our package, last checked 2026-10-25. Confirm at the [GA SOS fee page](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/filing-fees-and-expedited-processing-document-filings).

**Bottom line:** if a seed round next year is a real plan, form the Delaware C-corp now. Converting later costs more in legal and tax work. Budget a few hundred dollars a year extra for the two-state setup, and don't expect any tax savings.

**Bring in:** a startup corporate attorney for formation, founder stock with vesting (and file your **83(b) elections within 30 days**), and a CPA for the franchise tax filing.
