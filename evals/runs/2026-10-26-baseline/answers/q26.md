General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Short version:** pay the intern, and don't run payroll monthly.

**1. Don't use the GA DOL handbook's intern test.** The GA DOL handbook (DOL-224, R-11-24) still prints an older six-factor test. The federal standard since January 2018 is the **"primary beneficiary" test** in [WHD Fact Sheet #71](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/71-flsa-internships), which I re-checked live today. An intern at a for-profit company can go unpaid only if the *intern*, not the company, is the primary beneficiary. The test weighs seven factors:
1. Both sides understand the internship is unpaid.
2. The training is similar to what a school would provide.
3. The internship is tied to coursework or academic credit.
4. It fits the academic calendar.
5. It lasts only as long as it's useful for learning.
6. The intern's work complements paid staff rather than displacing them.
7. There's no promise of a job at the end.

**An intern who "ships real features" fails this test on its face.** That's productive work that benefits you and replaces engineering you'd otherwise pay for. Treat them as an **employee**:
- Pay at least **$7.25/hour**, plus overtime for hours over 40 in a week, or pay a computer-employee rate of **$27.63/hour or more**.
- Do a W-4, G-4, and I-9.
- Report the new hire to Georgia within 10 days.
- Include them on your workers' comp policy.

Market-rate pay for Georgia Tech software interns is far above the minimum anyway. If the student is on an F-1 visa, work authorization usually has to come through Georgia Tech before they start. That's outside these packages, so check with the school's international office.

**2. Monthly payroll isn't a safe choice in Georgia.** O.C.G.A. § 34-7-2 requires pay periods that split the month into at least **two equal parts**, meaning semimonthly or more often. DOL's [state payday table](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/payday) lists Georgia as semimonthly, re-checked live today. The statute excludes department heads and officials paid at stipulated monthly or annual salaries. Whether that exclusion covers your regular developers isn't settled, and it doesn't cover an intern. Any payroll provider can run semimonthly or biweekly at little or no extra cost, so this simplifies nothing. **Run payroll semimonthly or biweekly.**

**Sources:** the federal figures ($7.25 and $27.63) were re-checked against DOL today. Georgia statute text is from FindLaw's copy, current as of March 2024. If you still want an *unpaid*, credit-bearing internship, talk to an employment attorney first. Document all seven FS71 factors with Georgia Tech before the start date, and keep the intern off production work.
