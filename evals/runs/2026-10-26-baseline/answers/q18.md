Congrats on first revenue. **How you pay yourself depends on how your company is taxed**, so I need one thing from you first: **is it a single-member LLC, a multi-member LLC, an S-corp, or a C-corp?** (Your EIN letter, or any Form 2553 or 8832 you filed, will tell you.) Here's how it works for each:

| Taxed as | How you get paid | Payroll for you? |
|---|---|---|
| Single-member LLC | Owner's draws: transfer money from the business account whenever you want | No. The LLC isn't your employer |
| Multi-member LLC (partnership) | Guaranteed payments and/or distributions | No. Partners aren't W-2 employees ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/partnerships)) |
| S-corp | A "reasonable" W-2 salary, then distributions | Yes |
| C-corp | W-2 salary | Yes |

**If you're an LLC (the most common case at this stage)**
- **You're taxed on profit, not on what you take out.** Draws aren't a deduction or a separate tax. You owe income tax plus self-employment tax on your share of the profit, whether you take the cash or leave it in the business.
- **Self-employment tax** is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net earnings, once net earnings reach $400 ([IRS Topic 554](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554), checked today). The Social Security part stops at the 2026 wage base of $184,500 ([IRS Topic 751](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751), checked today).
- **Pay quarterly estimates,** because nothing is being withheld:
  - **Federal (1040-ES):** generally required if you'll owe $1,000 or more. The next payment is due **January 15, 2027** for Q4 2026.
  - **Georgia (500-ES):** same dates ([GA 500-ES](https://dor.georgia.gov/500-es-individual-and-fiduciary-estimated-tax-payment-voucher)).
  - *I didn't re-check those dates and thresholds live today.* They're from the 2026 Form 1040-ES, last verified 2026-10-25.
  - Pay through [IRS Direct Pay](https://www.irs.gov/payments/direct-pay) and the [Georgia Tax Center](https://gtc.dor.ga.gov/_/). You log in yourself.
- **Set aside a fixed share of each draw for taxes.** Your CPA can tell you the right percentage.

**Should you switch to S-corp?**
- **Not yet, probably.** $8K/month is revenue, not profit. An S-corp election only helps once profit comfortably covers a reasonable salary for you plus the extra costs of payroll, a 1120-S, and a GA 600S.
- **How the savings work:** you pay Social Security and Medicare only on the salary, not on distributions. The IRS judges "reasonable" by your duties, hours, and what comparable businesses pay. There's no official percentage ([IRS S-corp compensation](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues), checked today).
- **Timing:** for a calendar-year company, the election (Form 2553) is due by March 15 of the year it takes effect.
- **Have a CPA model it** with your real profit before you file.

**If you're a C-corp:** you're a W-2 employee of your own company. That means real payroll with withholding, and you'd register with the IRS, Georgia DOR, and Georgia DOL. Tell me and I'll walk you through it.

**Do now, whatever the entity:** pay yourself only from the business bank account, and label each transfer (draw, distribution, loan repayment, or payroll).
