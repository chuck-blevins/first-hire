General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Yes, Georgia is at-will, but don't cite that PDF.** The State Accounting Office "At-Will Employment" document is a policy for *state government* employees. It isn't Georgia law, and it doesn't govern a private startup. The rule you're after is the statute, O.C.G.A. § 34-7-1: "An indefinite hiring may be terminated at will by either party" ([FindLaw copy, current as of 2024-03-28](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-7-1/)). You don't need to cite any law in a termination letter. A short, factual letter is better: last day, the reason (sustained performance problems, with the dates of prior warnings), and what she'll receive.

**At-will has limits.** You can't fire someone for an illegal reason, such as retaliation for a complaint about pay or safety, for asking for an accommodation, or for jury service. Georgia's age-discrimination statute applies at any company size. If she recently complained, took leave, or is 40 or older, talk to an employment attorney before you act. Either way, collect your documentation (warnings, reviews, examples) first.

**What you owe her:**
1. **Separation Notice (DOL-800), on her last day.** You must give it to every separated worker, either electronically to her work email or as a signed paper copy. If she's unavailable, send it within 3 days by email or U.S. mail ([Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 300-2-7-.06](https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/300-2-7), checked 2026-10-26; [form](https://dol.georgia.gov/document/separation-notices/separation-notice-individual-dol-800/download)). State the reason "fully and clearly," and make it match what you tell her.
2. **Final wages.** Federal law doesn't require immediate payment ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/wages/lastpaycheck)). We found no Georgia final-paycheck statute. Paying on the next regular payday is the common practice, but we've only seen that in secondary sources, so treat it as unverified. Make no deductions without her written authorization.
3. **Unused PTO.** Pay it out only if your written policy or offer letter promises it.
4. **Severance.** It isn't required. If you offer it, trade it for a signed release, and have an attorney draft the release.
5. **Benefits.** With fewer than 20 employees, federal COBRA doesn't apply. If you have an insured health plan, Georgia state continuation may apply, so ask your carrier.
6. **Equity.** Unvested equity is usually forfeited under the plan. Vested options usually have to be exercised within about 90 days, so check her grant.

**Unemployment.** Poor performance on its own is usually *not* "misconduct" under Georgia rules, so expect her to qualify for benefits. If GDOL sends you a claim (DOL-1199FF), answer it by the date on the notice, usually 10 days, through [SIDES](https://dol.georgia.gov/use-employer-separation-sides-e-response). A late answer can raise your UI tax rate.

**Same day:** cut off all her system access, collect company devices, and remind her in writing of her confidentiality and IP obligations. Keep her I-9 until the later of 3 years after hire or 1 year after termination ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/form-i-9-resources/handbook-for-employers-m-274/100-retaining-form-i-9)).

*Not verified on an official source:* the Georgia final-paycheck timing and the PTO payout rule. Both come from secondary sources. An employment attorney can confirm them for an hour's fee.
