General information, not legal or tax advice.

**First, a correction:** your GA DOL handbook is out of date on this point. Since *Bostock v. Clayton County* (2020), the Supreme Court has held that Title VII's ban on sex discrimination covers sexual orientation and transgender status ([EEOC: Supreme Court decisions](https://www.eeoc.gov/history/selected-supreme-court-decisions-2000-2023)). Once you're covered by Title VII at 15 employees, firing, refusing to hire, harassing, or paying someone differently because of sexual orientation is illegal under federal law. Federal agencies' enforcement priorities shift, but *Bostock* is binding precedent and employees can sue. Don't rely on that handbook for federal law. It also has outdated overtime figures.

**What kicks in at 15**
- **Title VII, ADA, GINA:** no discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, and gender identity), national origin, disability, or genetic information. The ADA adds a duty to reasonably accommodate disabilities.
- **Georgia's disability statute** also applies at 15 ([O.C.G.A. § 34-6A-2](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-6a-2/)).
- **Do now (good practice, not a statute):** adopt an anti-harassment policy with a complaint channel, set up an accommodation process, and train your managers.

**What kicks in at 20**
- **ADEA:** no age discrimination against workers 40 and older.
- **COBRA:** you must offer continuation of your group health coverage. COBRA replaces Georgia's shorter state continuation rule.
  - **When it applies:** it's based on having 20+ employees on more than half of your typical business days in the **prior** calendar year, with part-timers counted as fractions ([26 CFR 54.4980B-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/54.4980B-2)).
  - **What to do:** if you cross 20 in 2027, COBRA likely applies from 2028. Set up administration with your carrier or PEO before then.

**How the count works:** you hit 15 or 20 once you have that many employees "for each working day in twenty or more calendar weeks" in this year or last ([EEOC coverage](https://www.eeoc.gov/employers/coverage-businessprivate-employers), checked today).
- **Who counts:** part-timers and remote staff in other states.
- **Who doesn't:** contractors.
- **Timing:** going to 17 this quarter doesn't make you covered tomorrow, but you'll get there within about 20 weeks. Get ready now.

**Already applies at your size (check these)**
- **Georgia E-Verify:** required once you have **more than 10 employees working 35+ hours a week**, counted on January 1. You provide your E-Verify number when you renew your business license. If you had 11+ full-timers on Jan 1, 2026, you should already be enrolled ([O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-36-local-government/ga-code-sect-36-60-6/); enroll at [e-verify.gov](https://www.e-verify.gov/)).
- **Georgia equal pay for equal work (10+ employees):** no sex-based pay differences for comparable work. Post the DOL-4107 notice.
- **Equal Pay Act:** applies to virtually all employers.
- **Workers' comp:** required since you hit 3.

**Next lines to plan for:**
- **25:** Georgia kin-care leave, if you offer sick leave.
- **50:** FMLA and the ACA employer mandate.
- **Remote staff:** their states may add their own thresholds.

**Bring in an employment attorney** before you cross 15 to review your handbook, policies, and offer letters.

*Georgia statute text above is from FindLaw's copy, current as of March 28, 2024. The package last verified these thresholds 2026-10-25.*
