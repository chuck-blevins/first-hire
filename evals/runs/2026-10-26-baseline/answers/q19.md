General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Short answer:** Yes, put yourselves on payroll. As S-corp shareholders who work in the business, you two are W-2 employees and run through payroll exactly like your new hire. You each need to take a "reasonable" salary before taking distributions. There's no IRS formula for it, so set it from market data and document how you got there.

**Putting yourselves on payroll alongside her**
- **Use the same accounts and process for all three of you:**
  - The LLC's EIN.
  - EFTPS for federal deposits ([eftps.gov](https://www.eftps.gov)). Enroll now, because the PIN comes by mail.
  - GA withholding through the [Georgia Tax Center](https://gtc.dor.ga.gov/_/).
  - A GA DOL unemployment insurance (UI) account through the [GA DOL Employer Portal](https://eresponse.gdol.ga.gov/idp/sso/employer/login).
  - You log in to each of these yourself. Or a payroll provider sets them up and files for you, but you remain liable for the tax.
- **Each of you fills out a W-4 and G-4.**
- **Taxes on each salary:**
  - **FICA:** 6.2% Social Security on wages up to $184,500 for 2026, plus 1.45% Medicare, withheld from you and matched by the company ([IRS Topic 751](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751), checked today).
  - **FUTA:** effectively 0.6% on the first $7,000 per person. Georgia wasn't a credit-reduction state as of 2026-10-25. The final 2026 list comes after Nov 10, and I couldn't confirm it live today.
  - **Georgia UI:** officers of an LLC taxed as a corporation are covered employees ([GA Rule 300-2-7-.12](https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/300-2-7)). New employers pay 2.70% of the first $9,500 per employee ([GA DOL FAQ](https://dol.georgia.gov/faqs-employers/employers-faqs-unemployment-insurance), checked today). That rate includes a 0.06% assessment that's set to expire Dec 31, 2026. The 2027 rate will be on your DOL-626 notice.
- **Workers' comp kicks in with this hire.** Georgia requires it once you regularly employ 3 or more people, and LLC members and officers count, so you two plus her makes 3. A founder can opt out of their own coverage with Form WC-10 but still counts toward the 3 ([SBWC](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/employer-information), checked today). Line up the policy before her start date.
- **Health insurance:** premiums the company pays for a more-than-2% shareholder go in W-2 box 1 only, not the Social Security and Medicare boxes. Tell your payroll provider.

**What salary to take**
- **The IRS factors** are your training and experience, duties, hours, what you pay non-shareholder staff, and "what comparable businesses pay for similar services" ([IRS S-corp compensation](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues), checked today).
- **The IRS can reclassify** distributions as wages if salaries are too low.
- **Practical guardrails:**
  - Benchmark each founder's role (CEO, CTO, and so on) against market pay for a company your size.
  - Keep a short memo explaining how you set the number.
  - Don't pay yourselves less than you're paying your first employee for comparable work. That's an easy red flag.
- **If cash is tight:** a lower salary can be defensible early on, but taking distributions while paying little or no salary is what draws IRS scrutiny.
- **Watch out if you raise money.** S-corps can't have preferred stock or fund shareholders, so a priced VC round usually means converting to a C-corp first.

**Bring in a CPA** to set the salary figures and to decide on Georgia's pass-through entity tax election. Bring in an insurance agent for workers' comp and the WC-10.
