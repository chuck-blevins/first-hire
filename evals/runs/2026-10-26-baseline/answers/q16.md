General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Short answer:** Texas is one of the lighter states to add. It has no state income tax, so there's nothing to withhold. You do have to register for Texas unemployment tax and decide about workers' comp. For the I-9, have someone check her documents in person on your behalf, or enroll in E-Verify and do it over video.

**What to set up in Texas**
1. **Unemployment tax with TWC.** You become liable once you pay $1,500 in gross wages in a calendar quarter, or have one employee in 20 different weeks of a year. You must register within 10 days of becoming liable ([TWC](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-tax/determine-tax-account), checked today). Her wages go to Texas unemployment insurance (UI), not Georgia's, because all of her work is localized in Texas.
2. **No state income tax withholding.** Georgia doesn't apply either: Georgia withholds from nonresidents only for work done in Georgia. That changes if more than 5% of her wages, or more than $5,000, comes from work in Georgia (GA DOR 2026 Employer's Tax Guide pp.10, 15; last verified 2026-10-25).
3. **Workers' comp is optional in Texas** for most private employers. If you don't carry it, you must report that to the state and report qualifying injuries to DWC ([TDI](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employer/index.html), updated 6/8/2026). Your Georgia policy doesn't automatically cover her, so ask your agent to add Texas.
4. **New-hire report** to Texas. Or register with HHS as a multistate employer and report everyone to one state ([ACF](https://acf.gov/css/employers/employer-responsibilities/new-hire-reporting)).
5. **Company-level Texas tax (ask a CPA).** A remote employee can make you "doing business in Texas" for franchise tax. Reports with revenue under the $2,650,000 no-tax-due threshold (2026–2027 reports) owe no tax but may still have to be filed ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/), checked today). Texas foreign-entity registration may also apply. I haven't verified Texas's test.
6. **Federal rules and your headcount.** Federal rules don't change for a remote hire, and she counts toward your headcount (you're at 7).

At your stage, a multi-state payroll provider or PEO will do the TWC registration and filings. Confirm in writing what it files for you.

**I-9 for someone you'll never meet**
She completes Section 1 by her first day, and Section 2 is due within 3 business days of her start date. There are two lawful ways to do Section 2 remotely:
- **Authorized representative.** Anyone you choose, such as a notary acting as your agent or a trusted contact in Austin, examines her original documents in person and completes Section 2. You remain liable for their mistakes ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/completing-form-i-9/completing-section-2-employer-review-and-attestation)).
- **DHS alternative procedure (video).** This is available only if you're enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing:
  - She sends copies of her documents, then shows the same originals on a live video call.
  - You keep the copies and note "alternative procedure" in Section 2.
  - You must use E-Verify for new hires at that site.
  - If you offer video exams at a site, offer them consistently there ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/remote-examination-of-documents), page updated 09/26/2025, checked today).

Enroll in E-Verify at [e-verify.gov](https://www.e-verify.gov/). You'll need it in Georgia anyway once you have more than 10 full-time employees.

**Bring in a pro:** a CPA for Texas franchise tax and registration, and your insurance agent for workers' comp.
