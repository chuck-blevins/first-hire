General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Yes, you really need a 409A, and almost certainly a fresh one after your seed.** If an option's strike price can be below fair market value (FMV) on the grant date, it's treated as deferred compensation. If it breaks the 409A rules, the holder owes tax on the income right away, plus a **20% additional tax** and interest ([26 U.S.C. §409A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/409A)). That penalty lands on your engineer, which is a terrible first-hire experience.
- **Safe harbor:** an independent appraisal no more than **12 months** old at the grant date ([26 CFR 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.409A-1), checked live 2026-10-26).
- **A pre-seed valuation is likely stale.** A $2M raise is the kind of material event a valuation should reflect, even if the old one is under 12 months.

**Checklist before next week's grant:**
1. **Equity plan:** board-adopted, and approved by stockholders within 12 months if you want ISOs ([26 U.S.C. §422(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/422)). Your seed docs may have created or expanded the pool, so confirm with counsel.
2. **409A valuation** from an independent firm, dated after the seed closed. These typically take a couple of weeks, so the grant may need to slip. That's normal. Don't grant on a guessed price.
3. **Board approval of the grant:** recipient, share count, ISO or NSO, strike price at or above the 409A FMV, vesting (commonly 4 years with a 1-year cliff), and any acceleration. Double-trigger acceleration is far more common than single-trigger ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/what-are-single-and-double-trigger-acceleration-and-how-do-they-work/), secondary).
4. **Grant agreement signed,** with a copy of the plan delivered. Grants under a written compensatory plan rely on SEC Rule 701 ([17 CFR 230.701](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/230.701)).
5. **Cap table updated:** founders, pool, SAFEs or preferred, and this grant.
6. **ISO limit:** ISOs that first become exercisable in a calendar year are capped at **$100,000** of stock (grant-date FMV). Anything above that is treated as an NSO ([§422(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/422), checked live).

**How much equity is normal?** These are market norms, not rules, and they come from secondary data I couldn't fetch live (Carta blocks automated access, so treat them as rough):
- **First hire:** a median of about **1.49% of fully diluted shares** for hire #1, dropping to about **0.3% by hire #5–6** ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-first-hire-equity-benchmarks-by-sector/), 2023–24 data).
- **Seed-stage option pool:** about **10–15%** fully diluted, sized to 12–18 months of hiring ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/option-pool/)).

Seniority, cash salary, and how "founding" the role is all move the number. Pricing it against your actual hiring plan is a conversation for your board.

**Bring in:** your corporate counsel (plan, board consent, grant docs) and a 409A valuation firm.
