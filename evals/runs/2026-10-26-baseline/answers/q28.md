General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Health insurance: no, you don't have to offer it.** The federal ACA employer mandate starts only at **50 full-time employees, including full-time equivalents** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/determining-if-an-employer-is-an-applicable-large-employer)). Georgia has no employer mandate of its own. That second point comes from secondary sources only.

**What a QSEHRA is.** A Qualified Small Employer HRA lets you give employees a fixed allowance. You then reimburse, tax-free, the individual health insurance premiums and medical costs they pay themselves. Key rules:
- **Who can offer one:** employers under 50 full-time employees and FTEs that offer **no group health plan to anyone**. A health FSA counts as a group health plan for this purpose.
- **2026 caps:** **$6,450 self-only / $13,100 family** per year, prorated for partial years ([IRS Pub 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b), re-checked live today).
- **Same terms for everyone eligible.** Amounts can vary by age and family size. You can exclude people with under 90 days of service, people under 25, and part-time or seasonal workers.
- **Employees must have their own qualifying coverage** to get reimbursed tax-free.
- **Give written notice 90 days before the plan year**, or when a new hire becomes eligible.
- **The allowance reduces any Marketplace premium tax credit** the employee gets ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/), re-checked today). For a lower-income employee, a QSEHRA can sometimes leave them worse off, so run the numbers with them.
- **It fits a 3-person team** because it's cheap to administer and you set the budget.
- **Alternatives:** an ICHRA, now marketed as a "CHOICE Arrangement," has no dollar cap. A small-group plan through Georgia Access SHOP may qualify you for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit.
- **If you're an S-corp or LLC**, owner participation follows different rules, so tell me your entity type.

**Paid sick days: no, Georgia doesn't require them.**
- **No statewide mandate.** There's no state law requiring private employers to offer paid sick leave or vacation.
- **No local mandates either.** State law preempts city and county benefit mandates ([O.C.G.A. § 34-4-3.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-4-3-1/)).
- **"Kin care" law ([§ 34-1-10](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-34-labor-and-industrial-relations/ga-code-sect-34-1-10/), re-checked today):** it applies only at **25+ employees**, and it says it doesn't require you to offer sick leave.
- **No general federal mandate** for private employers.

Employees outside Georgia may be covered by their own state's leave law. You'll still want a written PTO policy covering accrual, carryover, and payout at exit.

**What does apply to you now: workers' comp.** Georgia requires it once you regularly employ **3 or more**, and as a corporation's officer or an LLC member, **you count toward the 3, even if you waive your own coverage** ([SBWC FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs), re-checked today). At 3 people including you, you're likely already over the line. Fines run $500–$5,000, so get a policy now if you don't have one.

**Sources:** Georgia statute text is FindLaw's copy, current as of March 2024. For choosing between a QSEHRA, an ICHRA, and a group plan, talk to a Georgia-licensed benefits broker or a Georgia Access-certified agent.
