*General information, not legal or tax advice.*

**Georgia withholding is a flat 4.99% now, not graduated.** The DOR web page you saw is out of date. It still says "graduated scale" and links to a **2014** guide (checked 2026-10-26).

The current source is the [2026 Employer's Tax Guide](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2026-employers-tax-guide-updated-june-2026/download), revised Sept 2026. It says Georgia moved "from a flat rate of 5.19% to a flat rate of 4.99%," and employers could start withholding at 4.99% on **May 11, 2026** (p.3).

**How the calculation works (percentage method, guide p.51)**
1. Start with taxable wages for the pay period.
2. Subtract the standard deduction for the employee's G-4 filing status:
   - $15,000 a year for Single, Head of Household, or Married Filing Separately
   - $30,000 a year for Married Filing Jointly
3. Subtract $5,000 a year for each dependent allowance claimed.
4. Multiply what's left by 4.99%.

**Rough example for your $70K hire.** Assume Single with no dependents:
- ($70,000 − $15,000) × 4.99% ≈ **$2,745 a year**
- That's about **$229 a month**, or about $106 per biweekly paycheck.

Your hire's actual number depends on their **Form G-4**, so treat this as a ballpark ([G-4](https://dor.georgia.gov/g-4-employee-withholding), Rev. 06/03/26). If they don't turn in a G-4, you can use their W-4 election if it gives you enough information. If it doesn't, withhold as Single with zero allowances.

You can also skip the math. The guide has wage-bracket tables (pp.31–50) that give the same result, and any payroll provider will apply the rate automatically.

**Related things to set up**
- **Register for a GA withholding number** in the [Georgia Tax Center](https://gtc.dor.ga.gov/_/) before the first paycheck. DOR assigns your filing and payment frequency when you register.
- **Watch the e-pay threshold.** Once a G-7 return shows more than $500 withheld, you have to pay and file electronically from then on.
- **Federal withholding is separate.** It's based on the employee's W-4. Also remember FICA: 7.65% comes out of the employee's pay and you match it.

**Heads-up for January:** check the 2027 guide when DOR publishes it. The flat rate has changed by law in each of the last few years.
