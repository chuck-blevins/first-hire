General information, not legal or tax advice.

**Have an employment attorney draft this.** Several parts of your plan need changes. (This assumes the engineer works in Georgia. If not, that state's law governs.)

**1. The release must meet OWBPA rules because he's 52 and you have 20+ employees.** The ADEA covers employers with 20 or more employees ([EEOC](https://www.eeoc.gov/age-discrimination)). For his age-discrimination waiver to be valid, the release must ([EEOC waiver Q&A](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/qa-understanding-waivers-discrimination-claims-employee-severance-agreements), checked 2026-10-26):
- be written in plain language and refer specifically to the ADEA
- advise him **in writing** to consult an attorney
- give him **21 days** to consider it, and **7 days** to revoke after signing. Pay severance only after the revocation period ends.
- not waive future claims
- not bar him from filing an EEOC charge or taking part in an EEOC proceeding.

If anyone else is let go in the same program, the period becomes **45 days**, and you must give written disclosures of the decisional unit and the ages and job titles of the people selected and not selected.

**2. Document why this role was eliminated.** A "role eliminated" exit for a 52-year-old is a common age-claim fact pattern. Write down the business reason and the selection criteria now, and make sure no one replaces him in the same job under a new title.

**3. Narrow the confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses.** The NLRB's *McLaren Macomb* (2023) decision says broad versions of these clauses in severance agreements violate federal labor law when offered to non-supervisory employees. The Board applied it again on 2026-04-07 in *Prime Communications*, 374 NLRB No. 88. Two members said they're open to reconsidering it, but it remains current precedent ([NLRB summary](https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-april-6-10-2026), checked 2026-10-26). Here's how to narrow them:
- Limit confidentiality to the severance terms and trade secrets.
- Limit non-disparagement to defamatory statements.
- Add carve-outs for talking with government agencies and for protected workplace discussions.

**4. Shorten the non-compete. Three years is presumed unreasonable in Georgia.** Under O.C.G.A. § 13-8-57, a restraint of 2 years or less is presumed reasonable, and one longer than 2 years is presumed unreasonable ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-13-contracts/ga-code-sect-13-8-57/), checked 2026-10-26). Georgia non-competes also bind only certain employees: sales and customer-facing staff, managers, and key employees or professionals. Whether a given engineer qualifies is a judgment call for your attorney. A customer non-solicit or a narrow non-compete of 12 months or less is far more defensible. The FTC's non-compete ban is **not** in effect ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule)).

**5. Other things you owe him:**
- **DOL-800 Separation Notice** on his last day. Give "lack of work / position eliminated" as the reason, and list the severance on it. Severance can delay his unemployment benefits for the weeks it covers.
- **Final wages** on the next regular payday. This is common practice. We haven't verified a Georgia statute that requires it.
- **COBRA:** with 20+ employees, federal COBRA applies. Notify your plan administrator within 30 days ([EBSA](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/an-employers-guide-to-group-health-continuation-coverage-under-cobra)).
- **Equity:** give him a statement of his vested options and his exercise deadline. ISOs exercised more than 3 months after he leaves are taxed as NSOs.

Severance tax withholding and any change to his exercise window should go through your CPA or corporate counsel.
