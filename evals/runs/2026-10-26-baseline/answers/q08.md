General information, not legal or tax advice.

**How bad is it?** It's common and you can fix it, but don't wait. From what you describe (a year at 40 hrs/week, working only for you, core product), these two devs are very likely employees under the IRS common-law control test. Georgia's unemployment test is stricter still: GA DOL treats anyone you pay as an employee unless you can prove they're free from your control *and* run an independent business ([IRS: Contractor or employee](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee); [GA DOL Employer Handbook](https://dol.georgia.gov/documents/employer-handbook)). Here's where you're exposed:

- **Federal payroll tax:** you owe the employer share of FICA, and you're liable for income tax you didn't withhold. You can't recover any of it from the devs. Reduced IRC §3509 rates may apply if you filed 1099s and didn't intentionally disregard the rules ([IRS Pub 15](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p15.pdf), p.15).
- **Georgia UI:** back contributions, late penalties, and interest.
- **Overtime (FLSA):** if they were paid hourly below **$27.63/hr**, they may be owed overtime plus an equal amount in liquidated damages. Developers paid a salary of at least $684/week, or hourly at $27.63 or more, generally fall under the computer-employee exemption ([DOL salary levels](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime/salary-levels), checked 2026-10-26).
- **IP, often the bigger diligence issue:** a contractor's code is *not* automatically yours. Without a signed written assignment, they may own what they wrote ([Copyright Office Circ. 30](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf)).

**Cleanup, in order:**
1. **Get signed IP assignments now** that cover all past work. Your lawyer can draft confirmatory assignments. This is usually what closes the diligence item.
2. **Talk to a CPA about the IRS Voluntary Classification Settlement Program (VCSP) *before* you switch them to W-2.** The sequence matters. Under VCSP you pay **10% of one year's employment-tax liability** (figured at the reduced §3509 rates), with no interest or penalties, and the IRS won't audit those workers' classification for prior years. Requirements (checked live at [IRS VCSP](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/voluntary-classification-settlement-program), 2026-10-26):
   - You consistently treated them as contractors.
   - You filed all required 1099s for them. **Did you file 1099-NECs for 2025?** If not, you're likely ineligible.
   - You're not under an IRS employment-tax audit or a DOL or state classification audit.
   - You file **Form 8952 at least 120 days before** the date you start treating them as employees.
   If VCSP doesn't fit, ask about Section 530 relief or simply correcting going forward.
3. **Convert them to W-2** with full onboarding: W-4, G-4, I-9, and a PIIA. Make sure you have a GA DOR withholding account, a GA DOL UI account, and workers' comp if you now have 3 or more employees, counting officers ([SBWC](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/employer-information)).
4. **Georgia UI is separate from VCSP.** VCSP doesn't settle state exposure. Have your employment attorney decide how to approach GA DOL.
5. **Document** how you're classifying each role going forward.

**Bring in:** an employment attorney (IP assignments, overtime exposure, GA DOL) and a CPA (VCSP math and timing). Your seed lawyer is the right person to coordinate both.
