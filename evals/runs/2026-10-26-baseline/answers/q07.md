Short version: **no** on the code, and **probably not** on the 1099. Both are fixable, but fix them before you raise money.

**1. You probably don't own the code yet.**
Paying for code doesn't transfer the copyright. Without a signed agreement, a contractor generally keeps the copyright in what he writes.
- **Why "work for hire" doesn't cover you:** that rule applies to independent contractors only for nine specific categories of commissioned work, and software usually isn't one of them.
- **What the law requires:** for a contractor's code to belong to you, he has to *assign* it to you in writing, and he has to sign it ([Copyright Office Circular 30](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf), pp.2–3; 17 U.S.C. § 204(a)).
- **Why the Slack thread doesn't help:** agreeing on a rate in Slack likely gives you, at most, an implied license to use the code. You don't own it. Investors check this IP chain of title in diligence.

**Fix it now, while the relationship is good.**
- Have a startup attorney draft an IP assignment covering **all past and future** work.
- Add confidentiality terms and a list of any prior inventions he's excluding.
- Get it signed before the next payment.

**2. The 1099 is likely a misclassification.**
You don't get to choose the label. The facts decide it ([IRS: contractor or employee](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee)). Your facts point toward employee:
- He works roughly full time, only for you.
- He works on your core product, with no defined end.
- He uses your Jira and GitHub as part of your team.

**Georgia's unemployment test is stricter than the IRS test.** Anyone you pay is presumed an employee unless he's free from your control *and* runs his own independent business. GA DOL treats misclassification as employer fraud (GA DOL Employer Handbook; O.C.G.A. § 34-8-35(f)).

**What misclassification can cost you:**
- Back employer payroll taxes and income tax you didn't withhold
- GA unemployment tax, penalties, and interest
- Overtime exposure if he's non-exempt

**3. Your options.**
- **Make him a W-2 employee.** This is cleanest if the setup stays as it is. The employer-side payroll tax adds roughly 8%. Onboarding means a W-4, G-4, I-9, a PIIA (which fixes IP going forward), a GA withholding account, and a GA DOL UI account.
- **Restructure as a true contract.** That means a scoped project, his own methods and tools, other clients, and paying per deliverable. This is hard to do honestly for a full-time MVP builder.
- **Either way, ask a CPA** whether past periods need cleanup. The IRS Voluntary Classification Settlement Program (Form 8952) is one route ([IRS VCSP](https://www.irs.gov/VCSP)).

**4. If he stays a contractor through year-end:**
- Get a **W-9** from him now.
- Send and file a **1099-NEC** if you paid him **$2,000 or more** in 2026. The threshold rose from $600 for 2026 payments ([1099-NEC instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)).
- The deadline is normally Jan 31. Jan 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so it moves to **Feb 1, 2027**.

**Get professional help with:**
- **Startup attorney:** the IP assignment, which is the top priority.
- **Employment attorney or CPA:** the classification decision and whether to clean up past periods.
